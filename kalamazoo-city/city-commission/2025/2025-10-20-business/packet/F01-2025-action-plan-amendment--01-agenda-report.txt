SUBJECT:        Program Year 2025 HUD Action Plan Substantial Amendment

RECOMMENDATION:

It is recommended the City Commission approve the 2025 Action Plan Substantial Amendment
to allocate CDBG and HOME funding to specific activities managed by nonprofit and housing
partners that align with the 2025-2029 Consolidated Plan.

BACKGROUND:

Attached to this memo is the recommended substantial amendment to the Program Year (PY) 2025
Action Plan. The Action Plan was approved by the City Commission on April 21, 2025, with the
understanding that funding would be assigned at a later date to specific organizations and activities.
Typically, the City Commission is used to seeing HUD Action Plans that identify organizations, the
activities they intend to complete, and a detailed budget. However, the development of the 2025
Action Plan occurred at the same time as the new 2025-2029 Consolidated Plan. Staff wanted to
ensure the goals and priorities of the Consolidated Plan were approved before beginning any type of
application or proposal process that would help assign funding.

After the approval of the new Consolidated Plan, a request for proposal process was held for the
affordable housing priorities, which included single-family homeowner repairs, rental assistance, and
the HOME-required Community Housing Development Organization (CHDO) project. This process
garnered four proposals for single-family homeowner repairs, one proposal for rental assistance, and
one proposal for the CHDO funding, as follows:

In addition to the affordable housing proposal process, staff completed a request for proposals for
Neighborhood Solutions in the core neighborhoods. The city also received a request for funding
under the Housing Development Fund from Edison Community Partners to support their project
called Kalrecovery, which is permanent supportive housing for people recovering from substance
use disorder.

Staff reviewed the proposals for eligibility and risk under the HUD rules and determined all were
qualified for funding. However, the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) had
not yet approved the 2025-2029 Consolidated Plan or 2025 Action Plan, so the substantial
amendment was placed on hold. The Consolidated Plan, Annual Action Plan, and our CDBG and
HOME grant agreements were approved by HUD on September 16, 2025.

During this wait period, the city received an influx of program income ($431,178) from a previous
affordable housing loan. This revenue earned had to be booked as CDBG Program Income
according to funding guidelines. Per HUD and the city’s Citizen Participation Plan, this program
income will be allocated through this substantial amendment public process.

To ensure the substantial amendment included all the available CDBG and HOME funding, staff
completed a financial review. In addition to the program income previously noted, 2025 CDBG and
HOME funds are included in this substantial amendment, as well as funds available from previous
program years, and HOME dollars that were assigned to affordable housing activities that were still
under consideration.

     • Single-Family Homeowner Repair went from $703,807.14 to $550,000 due to the number of
       proposals received, their requested budgets, and expected outcomes in a 12-month period.
     • Rental Assistance went from $456,340 to $770,000. This was due to available HOME funds
       assigned under a previous Action Plan for the Affordable Housing priority. In addition, this
       recommendation is owing to the growing need for rental assistance in the community. Rent
       prices are increasing at a faster rate than incomes, pricing out many in the rental market.
     • CHDO funding increased from $109,880.41 to $150,000. While HUD requires that at least 15
       percent of our HOME funds are assigned to a CHDO project, the city can allocate more. As
       affordable housing remains a high priority, assigning some additional HOME funds already
       intended for this type of activity aligned with expected outcomes of the Consolidated Plan.
     • With the additional program income available under CDBG, funds were available to secure
       two of the neighborhood enhancement projects, increasing the award from $197,837 to
       $400,000. The Edison and Vine projects were both identified as having more immediate
       financial need. Supporting both of these neighborhoods with available PY2025 funds will
       better position future dollars for Eastside, Northside, and Stuart in coming years.

The total budget for the 2025 Program Year, including activities previously approved by the City
Commission on April 21, 2025, is provided in the attached Substantial Amendment.
