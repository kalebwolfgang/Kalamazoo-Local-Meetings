RECOMMENDATION:

It is recommended that the City Commission adopt a RESOLUTION authorizing the City Clerk
to sign an Intergovernmental Agreement for Early Voting Election Services Between Kalamazoo
County and Associated Municipalities.

BACKGROUND:

On November 8, 2022, Michigan voters approved Proposal 22-2 (Proposal 2), which amended
the Michigan Constitution to expand the ways voters can obtain, vote, and submit their ballots.
Among other provisions, Proposal 2 gives each Michigan voter the right to vote in statewide and
federal elections at an early voting site. Early voting sites operate like election day precincts,
where a voter can mark a ballot and place it in a tabulator. Early voting sites must be open for
nine (9) consecutive days, beginning two Saturdays prior to election day and continuing through
the Sunday immediately before election day. Under implementing legislation, jurisdictions have
three options for implementing early voting: 1) they can implement early voting on their own; 2)
multiple cities and/or townships can partner together through a “municipal agreement” to offer
an early voting site or sites; or 3) cities and townships can partner with the county clerk through
a “county agreement” to offer an early voting site or sites.

In the fall of 2023, 18 of the 19 jurisdictions in Kalamazoo County chose the third option and
entered into an early voting agreement with the County Clerk. The parties to this agreement
administered early voting for three federal elections in 2024. Based on that experience, 13
jurisdictions are choosing to enter into a cooperative agreement for the next two years.

Agreement Summary
The proposed agreement is very similar to the 2024-2025 early voting agreement. The agreement
empowers the Kalamazoo County Clerk to administer early voting for all participating
jurisdictions that are participating in the agreement. (At this time, 13 of the 19 cites and
townships in the county are planning to participate.) Although the County Clerk will be

The draft agreement and plan envisions the continued use of four (4) early voting sites in

voter can go to any site). The four proposed sites are: the Kalamazoo County Expo Center, the
City of Portage Parks and Recreation building near the Portage District Library, the Fetzer

agreement specifies the nine days of early voting required by statute as the days for early voting
in the county, and the early voting sites will be open from 9:00 a.m. - 5:00 p.m. each day. By
statute, the County Election Commission will approve the early voting sites and appoint the
election inspectors (poll workers). Local clerks will help recruit poll workers and serve as site
supervisors for the early voting sites. The City Clerk's Office volunteered to be the site
supervisor for the two locations in the City for a majority of the nine days of early voting (15 out
of 18 shifts).

Benefits
A countywide agreement is preferable to the City doing early voting on its own from both a
resource and service perspective. From a service perspective, participating in a county agreement
will give City voters more early voting sites to choose from compared to the City operating its
own sites. Voters from participating jurisdictions will be able to go to an early voting site that is
close to where they "do life," which could be where they live, shop, work, or go to school. And
unlike election day precincts, voters from participating jurisdictions will not need to worry about
being sent away because they went to the wrong location. This will be especially beneficial for
WMU students, who reside in multiple jurisdictions, including the City, Oshtemo Township, and
Kalamazoo Township.

From a resource and staffing perspective, early voting will require significant staff time,
regardless of how it is administered. Partnering with other clerks to do this work will reduce the
burden on each jurisdiction, including the Kalamazoo City Clerk's Office.

Engagement/Communication Tools
Under the provisions of the early voting agreement, the County Clerk will launch a
communication strategy to motivate, encourage, and build confidence with local voters regarding
participation in early voting. In addition, the Clerk's Office has budgeted for a postcard mailing
and will leverage the City's social media channels to communicate with voters about the several
options they have to cast their ballots.

FISCAL IMPACT:

Under the early voting agreement, costs will be divided among the participating jurisdictions on
a prorated basis using the percentage of registered voters. The City of Kalamazoo has 31% of the
registered voters in the county and will be responsible for 31% of the costs.There is one
exception to this formula: to secure a second early voting site within the City of Kalamazoo, the
City will pay 100% of the cost to administer that site, which is proposed to be at the Douglass
Community Center. Appendix C to the agreement includes the estimated costs. In the estimate,
the City is anticipated to pay $190,085 in operational costs over the two-year period of the
agreement. Included in the estimated cost are non-site specific charges to cover the county's
administrative expenses, including software, required public notices, and the cost for the early
voting coordinator position.

It is important to note that the costs presented with the agreement are estimates and are based on
the assumption that the state grant funding available in 2024 will not be available moving
forward. The City's cost for the agreement is presented as a "2 Year Total", but the bulk of those
costs will be realized in 2026, which is one fiscal year for the City of Kalamazoo. The proposed
FY2026 Budget for the City Clerk's Office includes $135,000 for the costs associated with this
early voting agreement.

It is estimated that the Clerk's Office would need to spend $314,286 to offer a comparable level
of service of three early voting sites to City voters for the 2026-2027 election cycle. Thus, it
makes sense from a funding standpoint for the City of Kalamazoo to partner with other
jurisdictions in the county to offer early voting.
