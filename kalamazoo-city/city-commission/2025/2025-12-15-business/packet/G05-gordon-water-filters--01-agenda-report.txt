RECOMMENDATION:

It is recommended that the City Commission approve a contract, and a budget amendment, with
Gordon Water Systems for the purchase of 350 mechanical filtration units for potable water and
1,400 replacement cartridge filter packs in the amount of $588,959.00.

BACKGROUND:

Public Services continues to work on a solution to improve water quality at Abbey 42. These
conditions date back to the original construction and installation of the oversized water main
serving the Abbey 42 apartment complex. This oversizing and construction agreement provided
for pipe upsizing to 12” diameter water from the originally designed 8” diameter water main.
The cost provided covered the difference in size. The oversized diameter was recommended by
Public Services to support future residential housing, commercial and industrial growth within
the area south of N Ave and north of E. O Ave within Pavilion Township. Construction occurred
in late 2023 and into the spring of 2024. The water main passed inspection and was placed into
service.

Corrective action in response to the Abbey 42 water quality complaints has followed industry
and Departmental standards and policies. Corrective actions to date have focused on corrective
responses to common themes associated with increased water age, water stagnation and
sedimentation, low chlorine residual, elevated concentrations of iron and manganese and
occasionally elevated levels of aluminum (above EPA secondary treatment standards, but below
health standards) and elevated concentrations of iron reducing bacteria and sulfur reducing
bacteria.

Contributing factors to these water quality complaints may include oversized public transmission
and distribution main that is presently underutilized, increased water age (also a factor of
oversizing), low chlorine residual (also a factor of oversizing and system operations), unknown
source of iron reducing bacteria originating within the newly constructed oversized main,
unknown source of sulfur reducing bacteria originating within newly constructed oversized main,
elevated concentrations of iron and manganese (above EPA secondary treatment standards, but
below health standards) originating within the newly constructed oversized main and additional
unknown contributing factors.

Specific corrective action to resolve these themes included the following:

• Flushing, continuous at times
• Installation of Hydrant Mounted Auto-Flushers (April 15 to Nov 15 use only)
• Installation of a Permanent Auto-Flusher (year-round use)
• Water Age Computer Modeling
• Closing valves within the High-Pressure District Distribution System to Decrease Water Age
• High-Pressure District System Operation Strategy Improvements
• Ice Pigging and Chlorination of the 12” Transmission Main from N Ave south to the
Abbey Loop
• Water Heater Flushing and Chlorination in Accordance with Manufacturer
Recommendations
• Increased Frequency Monitoring of Water Quality Parameters and Iron Reducing
Bacteria Monitoring
Aesthetic water quality concerns at Abbey 42 were showing improvement until around late June
to early July 2025. The following observations and/or events within the High-Pressure District
may have contributed to the recurrences and increased complaints of water quality concerns at
Abbey 42.
• Variable Frequency Drive (VFD) and Impeller Failure at Station No.6
• Inadequate Differential System Pressure to Operate the Pilot Valves at Station No.30
during High Peak Hour and Peak Day Demands
• Summer 2025 Peak Day and Peak Hour Demands Much Greater than Summer 2024
• Hydraulic Demands Requiring the Use of the Edgemoor Elevated Storage Facility
• Catastrophic Failure of a Private 12” Fire Service and Subsequent Fire Response at
Midlink Business Park Midlink BWA-Rescue-Fire
• Operational Failure of the Abbey Permanent Auto-Flusher Due to an Electrical Fault

Anytime conditions within the water system change related to flow, flow direction, and/or
pressure, there is increased potential for settled sediment and iron deposits within the mains to be
swept up and carried into the service lines and interior plumbing of the buildings and units. The
above-referenced events created disruption of the system while also preventing corrective action
strategies previously available in 2024.

Water Supply staff were alerted to the renewed concerns at Abbey 42 and meetings with
management were scheduled to identify the issues and work on solutions. Our first meeting of
now ongoing weekly to bi-weekly meetings occurred on July 24, 2025.

Immediate focus was directed at identifying what changes had occurred to cause these issues
while seeking to implement positive corrective action. The following actions were immediately
put into place:

• Manual Operations of the Abbey Permanent Auto-Flusher to Prevent any Further
Control/Electrical Faults
• Hydrant Flushing
• Installation of Hydrant Mounted Auto-Flushers (April 15 to Nov 15 use only)
• Seeking the Consulting Services of Tower Pinkster (Mechanical Engineer specializing in
Michigan and IBC Plumbing Code)
• Seeking the Consulting Services of Gordan Water (Laboratory Analysis for Additional
Parameters and Knowledge/Experience with Residential Water Treatment)
• Technical Assistance Request to the Kalamazoo County Health Department –
Environmental Health Division
• Technical Assistance Discussions with EGLE
• Ongoing Monitoring (Lab sampling) of Vacant Units, Service Entrances, Public Mains
• Resident Notifications

Public Services proposes immediate recommendations to support and fund improvements to
private plumbing facilities at Abbey 42 consisting of furnishing and installation of; mixing
valve(s) necessary to safely obtain storage temperatures of 140 degrees F within water heaters,
point of entry mechanical filtration of iron and manganese, turbidity monitoring, and externally
powered electric anodes.
