It is recommended that the City Commission approve a Payment in Lieu of Taxes (PILOT)
resolution to Kalrecovery I Limited Dividend Housing Association Limited Partnership
(LDHALP) for creation of 48 residential units pursuant to Section 35-4 of the Kalamazoo City
Code.

BACKGROUND:

       On October 16, 2023 a PILOT Resolution for Kalrecovery was approved. Due to
       lack of construction, on October 21, 2024 a subsequent PILOT Resolution for
       Kalrecovery was approved. The City of Kalamazoo PILOT Ordinance states that
       the approval of a PILOT resolution will remain in effect “provided that
       construction or rehabilitation of the housing development commences within one
       year from the effective date of the resolution.” Construction has been delayed as
       funding sources (capital stack) are being secured. Originally scheduled to begin
       construction in the fall of 2024, funding challenges proved to be difficult and
       present completion challenges. In 2023 Kalrecovery was awarded Tax Credits
       from Michigan State Housing Development Authority (MSHDA) Low Income
       Housing Tax Credit (LIHTC) Program yet the project continued to see
       construction and programmatic financial gaps. The recommended PILOT
       Resolution Approval is to ensure first quarter primary lender closing requirements
       are met.

      Applicant Organization & Legal Entity Type

      Hollander Development Corporation (HDC), the Kalamazoo County Problem-
      Solving Courts, Integrated Services of Kalamazoo (ISK), and the Michigan
      Association of Treatment Court Professionals (MATCP), are working to develop
      Kalrecovery, a permanent supportive housing (PSH) project for individuals in
      recovery from substance use disorders (SUDs). The project is anticipated to be
      owned by Kalrecovery I LDHALP, a limited dividend housing association limited
      partnership (“LDHALP”), with Hollander Development Corporation, or its
      designee, as the general partner. Hollander Development Corporation is a
      Kalamazoo-based affordable housing developer with a long track record of
      successful projects that benefit low- and moderate-income households, having
      developed over 4,000 housing units across Michigan since the company was
      established in 1979. HDC has a strong history of innovative practices and
      partnerships, incorporating sustainable design, early childhood learning, racial
      equity, community engagement, workforce housing, and other elements that
      further enhance the community benefits offered by affordable housing
      development.

      Project Scope, Budget & Timeline

      The Kalrecovery project continues Hollander Development’s record of
      developing creative housing solutions to meet community needs. The project
      brings together a broad range of partners to implement an innovative model for
      recovery housing focused on individuals and families that have been impacted by
      substance-use disorders. Upon completion, the development is anticipated to
      provide 46 housing units for low-income individuals and families; two additional
      apartments will accommodate on-site recovery coaches/site managers for a total
      of 48-units.

      Investment in the project is expected to total approximately $17.1 million.
      Sources of funding are anticipated to include Michigan State Housing
      Development Authority (MSHDA) and conventional financing, Low-Income
      Housing Tax Credit (LIHTC) equity, Kalamazoo County housing millage funds,
      energy efficiency and solar tax credit equity, utility rebates and incentives, and
      deferred developer fees. Funds will be used to acquire a long-vacant site owned
      by the City of Kalamazoo Brownfield Redevelopment Authority, prepare the
      property for safe redevelopment, construct 48 new housing units and space for
      supportive services, activate public space through installation of sidewalks, and
      place the property into service.

       Demographic analysis of participants in the Men’s and Women’s Drug Treatment Courts
       has shown that a substantial number of participants earn less than 30% of the area median
       income. It is also likely that many of the potential residents of the Kalrecovery project
       will meet the Department of Housing and Urban Development’s definition of Category 1,
       2, or 4 homelessness. To help address these dynamics, the development team will request
       MSHDA Project-based Vouchers (PBVs) for the recovery housing units.

       The MSHDA PBV program is a direct housing subsidy tied to a project rather than an
       individual household. Residents who qualify for the project pay 30% of their income
       towards housing expenses, with a nominal monthly payment for individuals with zero
       income. MSHDA PBVs are independent of the tenant-based Housing Choice
       Vouchers available to residents of Kalamazoo County; prospective residents with
       their own Housing Choice Vouchers will be welcomed in all units of the
       development.
