SUBJECT:       Adoption of an Ordinance to Amend Certain Sections of Chapter 38 of the
               Kalamazoo City Code to Change the Rates Charged for Drinking Water

RECOMMENDATION:

It is recommended that the City Commission adopt an ordinance to amend certain sections of
Chapter 38 of the Kalamazoo city code to change the rates charged for drinking water. The
ordinance amends Chapter 38 of the Kalamazoo Code of Ordinances to increase water system
revenues, effective January 1, 2026.

BACKGROUND:

In accordance with the 2026 water system budget, the City Administration recommends an
overall system revenue increase of 5% for water. In accordance with the STANTEC FAMS
Model and Cost of Service Analysis; the 5% structural rate increase results in a 6.5% rate
increase for Residential customers, 3.0% rate increase for Multi-Family customers, 5.0% rate
increase for Commercial customers, 2.0% rate increase for Industrial Customers and a 0% rate
increase for Seasonal customers. Approval and implementation of this increase will allow the
Utilities to continue to operate and maintain the system and make capital improvements as
outlined. Also, the new rates will work to improve reserve funds, debt coverage ratios and allow
for the continued stabilization of the Utility’s financial position. Continued annual rate increases
are needed to continue to support system wide capital needs and financial health over the next
several years.

Stantec was commissioned to complete a forecast of 2026 revenue requirements and a financial
asset management system (FAMS) model of financial performance over the next 5-year period.
The Utility Policy Committee voted in support of the 5% rate recommendation at their October
10th meeting.

October 31st Stakeholder Presentation

The Utility Policy Committee (UPC), a citizen board comprised of four (4) members appointed

Commission, was presented with these rate recommendations through many public meetings,
ultimately recommending the 5% rate increase at the September 11, 2025 meeting. The UPC
supports these rate recommendations.
