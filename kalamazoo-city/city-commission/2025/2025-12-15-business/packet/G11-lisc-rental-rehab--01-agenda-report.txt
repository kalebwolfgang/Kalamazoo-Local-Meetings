RECOMMENDATION:

It is recommended that the City Commission approve an agreement extension to Local Initiatives
Support Corporation for the 2023 grant award from the Coronavirus State and Local Fiscal
Recovery Funds (SLFRF).

BACKGROUND:

On August 21, 2023, the City Commission approved a grant to Local Initiatives Support
Corporation (LISC) for a rental rehabilitation program. This grant was for $1,500,000 to
rehabilitate approximately 48 rental units to increase the number of available to low-income
households holding rental assistance vouchers. The program provides funding to local property
owners/landlords for rehabilitation of existing rental units that they will then make available to
households receiving rental assistance.

The amended agreement had LISC completing the rental rehabilitations by December 31, 2025.
While LISC has made great strides in expending the funds, some of the units slated for
rehabilitation need additional time to complete construction. To date, LISC has completed the
following:

      • Funding has been assigned to 16 property owners, assisting a total of 48 units.
      • Of the 48 units, 24 have completed their rehabilitation and 24 are still under construction.
      • Of the 24 units under construction, 15 should be completed by the end of January.
      • The remaining 9 units will need until May to complete the expected work.

STRATEGIC VISION ALIGNMENT:

This agreement extension continues to support the following strategic vision goals as it results in
affordable housing:

    1. Economic Vitality

     • Property Management: Promotes the revitalization and redevelopment of existing
       properties to encourage sustainable economic growth.

    2. Safe Community

     • Proactively reinvest in neighborhoods through support of programs to help residents fix,
       rehabilitate, and stabilize homes through critical home repair assistance, education
       programs, and weatherization.

    3. Shared Prosperity

     • Family: Coordinate services to address basic needs and well-being, including
       transportation, housing, childcare, and financial capability.

COMMUNITY ENGAGEMENT:

Inform (one-way conversation) – no feasible alternatives available due to legal/regulatory
restrictions, standards and best practices, or resource limitations; the community will be made
aware of the project.

All potential projects funded through SLFRF dollars were evaluated against the five main City
Commission and Community Priorities. To create these priorities, the following public outreach
resources were utilized:

     • Community Survey (statistically significant) conducted every two years, with the last
       completed in 2021
     • ARPA/Stimulus Survey which was issued in June of 2021 and received 1,500 responses
     • 30-day public comment period on the ARPA funding
     • Feedback from City Commission during their annual retreats as to priorities
     • Imagine Kalamazoo 2025 and the engagement received in its creation

These sources were reviewed, and common themes discovered, which were brought back to the
City Commission for approval prior to allocation of SLFRF funds. The maintenance of quality
affordable housing as a priority was a result of this public outreach.
