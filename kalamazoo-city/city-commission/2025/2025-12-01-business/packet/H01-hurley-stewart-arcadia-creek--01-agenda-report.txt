It is recommended that the City Commission approve a contract with Hurley and Stewart for the
design of Arcadia Creek Phases One (Kalamazoo Avenue to West Main), Two (West Main to
Lovell), and Three (Lovell to Oliver) in the amount of $2,500,000 funded through the PROTECT

BACKGROUND:

The City of Kalamazoo’s stormwater system is a network of pipes and structures that collect
stormwater runoff from buildings, streets, parking lots, and adjoining lands, and carries it to a
designated location – in this portion, Arcadia Creek. Ultimately, all the runoff ends up in Lake
Michigan. The entire Kalamazoo watershed, including Arcadia Creek, drains to, and is
influenced by the Great Lakes. Anything that improves the quality of runoff in Kalamazoo will
benefit Lake Michigan and the entire Great Lakes system. Locally, as we improve the quality of
run-off and eliminate flooding, we protect vulnerable people in our underserved and
disadvantaged neighborhoods from a host of negative health and environmental consequences
(carried directly by that floodwater) and ensure both resilience and access.

Stormwater systems are designed to collect the surface water runoff and transfer it elsewhere to
help prevent flooding and damage along the city’s streets, businesses, and residential homes.
Undersized infrastructure and the resulting breaks, blockages, and erosion in this system
frequently affect our stormwater asset’s ability to serve its purpose and causes localized flooding
in heavy precipitation storms. This flooding creates both short-term impacts (lack of access and
crashes) as well as long-term impacts (the spreading of contamination/pollution, damage to roads
and structures, erosion). Establishing a system that can withstand increasing weather events to

Kalamazoo’s inadequate stormwater infrastructure causes drainage problems and localized
flooding during high precipitation storms in and around the Arcadia Creek drainage basin. In
2021, as part of the City of Kalamazoo’s asset management program for stormwater, the city
completed a stormwater capacity study, with the goal of identifying undersized pipes. A
hydraulic model was used to analyze pipe capacity, including diameter and flow direction. The
report identified pipes that should be considered for upsizing in conjunction with other roadwork
projects. The time is right for this work - this project fully aligns with work on Kalamazoo and
Michigan Avenues as it relates to the two-way street conversion and work surrounding the new
development at the corner of West Kalamazoo Avenue and North Westnedge Avenue and should
be completed in conjunction with these projects to protect these assets from flood damage in the
future.

Arcadia Creek Phases One-Three: The city will continue work to the west of downtown to
increase capacity (currently less than one third of what is needed) and modernize an aging and
failing stormwater infrastructure. The overarching goals of this work are to:

     • Daylight as much of the creek as possible to improve stream health and increase capacity,
     • Ensure any areas which cannot be daylighted are increased to required capacity
       (increasing the size of any assets which must be covered to address current and future
       needs),
     • Remove all infrastructure and development from the floodplain, and
     • Incorporate natural elements and design for multi-modal use of the area and connectivity.

Daylighting will provide opportunities to reoxygenate the creek’s flow and set the stage for its
eventual return to a natural state. This will also restore balance to the Kalamazoo River (of which
Arcadia Creek is a tributary), and eventually to Lake Michigan. This work will occur in three
phases:

     • Phase One: This phase will connect to the eastern end of current improvements and move
       upstream 1500 feet to West Main. There will be sections of concrete open channel similar
       to the 1993 improvements to the creek from Westnedge to Park Street, which
       successfully ended flooding in that area. Boulders and natural planting beds will
       naturalize the creek at normal flow and allow contained flooding to occur (causing no
       damage). A decorative lined basin with an adjustable weir outfall will be located between
       Elm Street, the railroad, and Michikal. Box culverts will be utilized for street crossings.
       Pervious pavers are proposed for the sidewalks.
     • Phase Two: This phase will connect to Phase One at West Main Street and continue
       upstream 2300 feet to Lovell Street. The size of the open channel will be increased to
       handle the 100-year storm flow. Box culverts will be installed under West Main and
       Academy Streets.
     • Phase Three: This phase will connect to Phase Two at the east end of Lovell Street and
       extend upstream 2000 feet to Oliver Street. This section runs mostly through Western

Detailed Explanation of Projected Changes: In addressing stormwater both in the Downtown
Kalamazoo Transportation Network and Arcadia Creek areas the city will achieve real and
tangible benefits, including the following:

     • Kalamazoo will experience improved transportation resilience through the elimination of
       a floodplain (and removal of over 70 structures and major streets from the current
       floodplain),
     • Residents of disadvantaged neighborhoods to the north and west will experience
       consistent and reliable connections to key resources in the Downtown and beyond,
     • Property values in these disadvantaged neighborhoods will increase (as a result of the
       floodplain elimination), supporting planned economic development and future
       development,
     • The project area will experience significant environmental benefits as a result of the
       naturalization of Arcadia Creek,
     • Users of non-motorized transportation will benefit from safer, improved, and intentional
       assets designed in conjunction with this project, supporting the reduction of emissions
       and use of active transportation, and
     • Users of motorized transportation will benefit from safer streets and additional routes (as
       a result of the two-way conversion), presenting more resilience and options during a
       disaster.

This design work is the first phase of the project and is funded through the PROTECT grant
received by the City of Kalamazoo in 2023. These changes meet the community’s goals of
streets that are safe and comfortable for all users. This work will have a profound impact on
economic vitality in and around Downtown and reconnect the Downtown with adjacent
neighborhoods.

Five proposals were received by the deadline given in the City’s publicly issued and advertised
Request for Proposal process; one of these proposals was deemed non-responsive. A
comprehensive project team was assembled to review and compare the four remaining proposals
using an MDOT approved qualifications-based selection method. Hurley and Stewart's proposal
had the highest cumulative score and was selected based on its conveyed understanding of the
project scope, the firm's experience, and its project delivery abilities.
