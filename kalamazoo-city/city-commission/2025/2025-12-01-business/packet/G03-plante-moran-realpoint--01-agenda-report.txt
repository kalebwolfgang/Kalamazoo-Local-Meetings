BACKGROUND:

The City receives applications from developers, nonprofits, and others for financial incentives
and grants, including discounts, grants, loans, and/or tax increment financing (TIF) through the
City’s Brownfield Redevelopment Authority (BRA) and/or the grants administered by the City’s
Community Development division, principally the HOME Investment Partnerships Program
(HOME) entitlement grant through the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development.

As a U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) entitlement grantee, the City
is required to conduct an underwriting and subsidy layering review of all proposed HOME
Investment Partnerships Program (HOME) funded projects before any commitment of federal
funds. Per 24 CFR 92.250(b), the City of Kalamazoo must evaluate projects to determine that an
owner or developer does not receive an unreasonable level of profit or return on their investment
and that the City does not invest any more HOME funds, alone or in combination with other
governmental assistance, than is necessary to provide quality affordable housing that is
financially viable for at least the minimum period of affordability required by HOME fund
investments. This must include an examination of the sources and uses of funds for projects to
determine that costs are reasonable and an assessment of the experience and financial capacity of
the developer, firm written financial commitments for the project, and the current market
demand of the neighborhood in which the project will be located. Consultants should thus be
experienced with the regulations found in the Uniform Administrative Requirements, Cost
Principles, and Audit Requirements for Federal Awards (2 CFR 200) and HOME program
underwriting, cost allocation and subsidy layering (24 CFR 92.250).

The City has a Brownfield Redevelopment Authority and operates under the Brownfield
Redevelopment Financing Act, PA 381 of 1996 as amended, and other local operational and
incentives policies. These policies and PA 381 require the City of Kalamazoo’s Brownfield
Redevelopment Authority to evaluate projects to determine if certain financing assumptions are
reasonable. These tests for reasonableness include aspects of a project such as estimated costs,
proposed housing subsidy and financing gap, and public financing sources. Furthermore, the
Authority’s local policies require an evaluation to ensure that the requested Tax Increment
Financing does not over-incentivize the project by creating an unreasonable rate of return on
investment than is necessary to realize the development. Experience with the regulations in PA
381 and all guidance documentation from the Michigan Economic Development Corporation
(MEDC); Michigan Department of Environment, Great Lakes, and Energy (EGLE); and
Michigan State Housing Development Authority (MSHDA) is necessary.

These required analyses had, previously, primarily been done by City staff. Starting last year
following a competitive bid process, the City contracted professional support from Plante Moran
Realpointe to provide real estate underwriting and third-party review of financial information
will help to ensure all funds are used in compliance with applicable laws and regulations. This
allows the City to better prioritize funding projects with the greatest likelihood of success and
benefit to residents.
