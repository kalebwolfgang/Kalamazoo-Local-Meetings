SUBJECT:       Adoption of a Restated Resolution Approving an Application for a Commercial
               Rehabilitation Exemption at 261 E Kalamazoo Avenue

RECOMMENDATION:

It is recommended the City Commission adopt the restated Resolution approving an application
for a Commercial Rehabilitation Exemption at 261 E Kalamazoo Avenue.

BACKGROUND:

The City Commission approved a Commercial Rehabilitation Exemption Certificate for 261 E
Kalamazoo Avenue on October 20, 2025, within the Speareflex Commercial Rehabilitation
District. Following the City’s approval, the application was submitted to the State Tax
Commission for final review. During its review, the State Tax Commission requested that the
resolution be restated to include items that were not referenced in the original resolution. The
major items that must be included in the resolution for state approval are shown in red on the
attached resolution, and stated here:
(1) WHEREAS, the taxable value of the property proposed to be exempt plus the aggregate
taxable value of the property previously exempt and currently in force under Public Act 210 of
2005 does not exceed 5% of the total taxable value of the City of Kalamazoo
(2) WHEREAS, the rehabilitation includes improvements aggregating 10% or more of the true
cash value of the property at commencement of the rehabilitation as provided by section 2(k) of
Public Act 210 of 2005.

No changes have been made to the project, the district, the terms of the exemption, or the City
Commission’s original action. This is a procedural step to ensure the resolution includes all
information requested by the State Tax Commission in order to complete its approval process.

The proposed restated resolution includes the same 10-year exemption term previously approved
and reflects the total project investment and expected tax impact as required for State Tax

COMMUNITY ENGAGEMENT:

Appropriate Depth of Engagement

Consult (two-way conversation) – the community will have a chance to react to the project
through two-way conversation.

State law governing Commercial Rehabilitation Exemption certificates requires a public hearing
before the local governing body, with notice provided to the property owner, taxing jurisdictions,
and the public. The City completed this process prior to adoption of the original resolution,
including publication of notice and a public hearing on October 6, 2025 where the public had the
opportunity to provide comment. The City’s Public Participation Plan reflects this level of
engagement for projects of this type, and the two-way conversation outreach strategies will be
relevant as the project moves through the development process. Because the current agenda item
is a corrective revision to the previously adopted resolution and does not change the scope or
exemption terms, no additional public notice or process is required.
