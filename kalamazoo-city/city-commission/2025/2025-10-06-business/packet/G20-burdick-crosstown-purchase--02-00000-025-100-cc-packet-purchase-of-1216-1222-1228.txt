(a) Buyer and seller are typically motivated;

consider their own best interests;

(c) A reasonable time is allowed for exposure in the open market;

(d) Payment is made in terms of cash in U.S. dollars or in terms of financial
arrangements comparable thereto; and

(e) The price represents the normal consideration for the property sold unaffected by
special or creative financing or sales concessions granted by anyone associated

with the sale.

PART IV ANALYSIS OF DATA AND CONCLUSIONS

Highest and Best Use

The highest and best use is that reasonable and probable use that will support
the highest present value. Highest and best use as defined by The Dictionary of Real
Estate Appraisal, 5'" ed., s.v. “highest and best use,” “highest and best use of land or a
site as though vacant,” and “highest and best use of property as improved.”

Highest and best use:

"The reasonably probable and legal use of vacant land or an improved
property, that is physically possible, appropriately supported, financially
feasible, and that results in the highest value. The four criteria the highest
and best use must meet are legal permissibility, physical possibility
financial feasibility, and maximum productivity. Alternatively, the probable
use of land or improved property-specific with respect to the user and
timing of the use-that is adequately supported and results in the highest
present value."

Highest and best use of land or a site as though vacant:

“Among all reasonable, alternative uses, the use that yields the highest
present land value, after payments are made for labor, capital, and
coordination. The use of a property based on the assumption that the
parcel of land is vacant or can be made vacant by demolishing any
improvements.”

Highest and best use of property as improved:

“The use that should be made of a property as it exists. An existing
improvement should be renovated or retained as is so long as it continues
to contribute to the total market value of the property, or until the return
from a new improvement would more than offset the cost of demolishing
the existing building and constructing a new one.”

Implied within these definitions is recognition of the contribution of that specific use to
community environment, or to community development goals in addition to wealth
maximization of individual property owners. It is also implied that the determinations of
highest and best use results from the appraiser's judgment and analytical skill, that is,
that the use determined from analysis represents an opinion of the appraiser's. The
highest and best use of both land as vacant, and improved, must meet four criteria. The
criteria are that the highest and best use must be (1) legally permissible, (2) physically
possible, (3) financially feasible, and (4) maximally productive.

Cost Approach

The Cost Approach is based on the Principle of Substitution that states that a
prudent investor would pay no more for a property than the cost of constructing a
property of equal desirability and utility without undue delay. This approach to value is
dependent upon the relationship between the physical object and Market Value often
referred to as Physical Value. Physical Value consists of the current Market Value of
the subject site as if vacant, to which is added the depreciated Market Value of the
improvements on the site and to the site. The latter is derived from the estimate of the
cost to reproduce or replace the improvements from which must be deducted accrued
depreciation, if any, in terms of physical deterioration, functional obsolescence, and
external obsolescence as defined previously in this report.

The Cost Approach was determined to not be an applicable methodology from
which to base an estimate of the subject property market value for the following reason:

e Owners of and investors in the subject property type generally equate bid prices

on a comparative per unit basis or the relationship between income and value,

and rarely rely on a cost analysis as a basis for market bids.
Therefore, the Cost Approach was not used as a method from which to base an opinion
of Market Value for the subject property.

for the comparable building sales are adjusted for differences between the comparable

PART IV ANALYSIS OF DATA AND CONCLUSIONS

Final Reconciliation

The reconciliation is the last step in the appraisal process in which the final
Market Value is estimated from the various estimates of Market Value developed by the
Cost, Sales Comparison, and Income Approaches. The relative significance,
applicability, and defensibility of each indicated value is analyzed, with the greatest
weight given to the approach deemed most appropriate for the subject property.

The analysis objective was to estimate the subject property Market Value of the
Fee Simple interest. The Cost and Income Approaches were not used to estimate the
Market Value given the subject property age, condition and location characteristics, and
lack of use as a basis for market bids by typical market participants for the subject
property type.

The Sales Comparison Approach valuation method was utilized for the market
value analysis. The Sales Comparison Approach benefits from a good supply of
comparable property sales. Potential owner occupants tend to place more emphasis on
comparative unit values when placing market bids.
