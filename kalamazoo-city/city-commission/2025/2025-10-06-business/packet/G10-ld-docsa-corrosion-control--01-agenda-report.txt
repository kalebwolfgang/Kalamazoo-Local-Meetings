$248,799.06.

BACKGROUND:

Currently, the City of Kalamazoo uses sodium hexametaphosphate and Carus 8400 for iron
sequestration and corrosion control in the drinking water system. In 2017, Tetra Tech, Inc.
completed a Desktop Corrosion Control Chemical Analysis which recommends a blend of
orthophosphate and polyphosphate for all pump stations in the City of Kalamazoo’s drinking
water system. In 2019, it was decided to move forward with implementing the Desktop
Corrosion Control Chemical Analysis to fully optimize corrosion control in the water system.
 The Michigan Department of Environment, Great Lakes, & Energy (EGLE) required Public
Services to perform additional studies to validate the desktop study that was completed. Public
Services and Tetra Tech, Inc. performed two rounds of solubility testing in 2020 and 2021 and
coupon rack testing in 2022. In February 2023, EGLE approved use of Carus 8700 based on the
results of the studies. Tetra Tech, Inc. completed the design of the Water System Corrosion
Control Improvements Project to upgrade all 13 treatment plants to Carus 8700. Bids were
opened for the project on January 10, 2024. The project was awarded to LD Docsa Associates,
Inc. in February 2024. Non-permit work began on the project as work continued with EGLE on
having the project permitted under Public Act 399 (PA 399), Michigan Safe Drinking Water Act.
 The PA 399 permit was issued on June 11, 2025.
