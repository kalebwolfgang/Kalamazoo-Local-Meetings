Fire Hydrants
When the heat index reaches 100 degrees, the City of Kalamazoo strategically opens twelve designated fire
hydrants to provide additional relief for residents. For safety reasons, residents should never attempt to open a
fire hydrant themselves. Unauthorized operation can negatively affect water pressure throughout the system,
potentially compromising firefighting efforts and public safety during an emergency. The Department of Public
Services will announce when hydrants are to be opened and available.

from the statewide Michigan Green Communities (MGC) program. It is the third straight year that Kalamazoo
has earned a gold ranking. Local governments are awarded bronze, silver, gold, or platinum seals of

City Budget Process
The development of the City’s annual budget is now underway, marking the beginning of a collaborative
process to establish funding priorities and allocate resources for the upcoming fiscal year. The budget serves as
the City’s primary policy document, translating community needs and organizational goals into a financial plan
that supports the delivery of essential services and strategic initiatives. City staff are working across departments
to evaluate service needs, operational requirements, capital investments, and long-term financial sustainability.
Equity remains an important consideration throughout the budget development process, with departments
being asked to evaluate how proposed investments and service levels support fair and inclusive outcomes for
all residents and higher weight attributed to equity in budget requests.

As part of this process, departments are reviewing existing programs and proposed projects to ensure
alignment with the vision and objectives outlined in the upcoming Imagine Kalamazoo 2035 (IK 2035) master
plan and the priorities established by the Kalamazoo City Commission. Staff are evaluating opportunities to
advance effective city operations, ensure excellent service delivery, build community trust and safety, strong
financial stewardship, and thoughtful investment in housing and neighborhoods. This alignment helps ensure
that budget decisions support the City’s long-term strategic direction while responsibly managing public
resources and addressing emerging community needs.

Boil Water Advisories
Boil Water Advisories are precautionary. They are issued while we test to confirm the water has not been
affected. Our team completes two separate rounds of testing before advisories are lifted. While under a Boil
Water Advisory, affected customers are advised to boil their tap water for two minutes before consuming it, or
use bottled water until the advisory is lifted. No special actions are needed for water used for hygiene. The
vast majority of Boil Water Advisories are issued out of an abundance of caution and lifted within three days.

The Kalamazoo Department of Public Services hand delivers water advisory notices to affected homes and
businesses whenever possible. If the affected area is large and includes a lot of properties, advisories may be
issued by media release. Here is a list of watermain breaks and boil water advisories for the weeks of June 1-

‘Kalamazoo & You’: Replacing Lead Lines, Strengthening Neighborhoods
The City of Kalamazoo has replaced more than two-thirds of its lead service lines, with more than 6,700
replacements completed as of the end of May, and the work continues. A key element of the City’s approach
has been a commitment to equity. The City is prioritizing lead service line replacements in neighborhoods most
affected by aging infrastructure, including areas that have historically been underserved, helping ensure that
all residents benefit from safer drinking water and improved public health protections.

In our public affairs video series, Kalamazoo & You, City of Kalamazoo Communications Manager Mike Smith
presents two segments focused on the City’s lead service line replacement program. In Part 1, Public Services
Director James Baker discusses the ongoing initiative and why it remains one of the City’s most important
infrastructure and public health projects. In Part 2, Assistant City Engineer for Water Resources Anna Crandall
explains the process, logistics, and public health importance of replacing aging lead service lines throughout
the city.

The Part 1 video on Facebook can be viewed here, and Part 2 can be viewed here. Be sure to check out
Public Media Network and the City’s Facebook page for previous and upcoming Kalamazoo & You videos.
