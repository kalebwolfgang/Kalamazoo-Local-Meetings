RECOMMENDATION:

It is recommended that the City Commission approve an 18-month contract extension with
Waste Management for the loading, transport, and disposal of biosolids in the amount of in the
amount of $20,892,000.

BACKGROUND:

Approximately 75,000 wet tons of biosolids are generated every year at the KWRP as part of
various wastewater treatment processes. The current method of disposal for biosolids produced
at the WRP is placement in regional landfills. This method of disposal requires loading and
hauling of these biosolids to these landfills for ultimate disposal. Generally these biosolids need
to be mixed with other municipal solid waste to ensure that proper compaction of waste materials
occurs in the landfill.

FISCAL IMPACT:

This contract reflects an updated price of $188.41 per wet ton for routine loading, hauling,
stabilization, and disposal of wastewater biosolids. Several industry-wide factors continue to
influence the cost of biosolids management.

Landfill managers across the country are experiencing reduced volumes of residential waste,
which is typically used to blend with centrifuged biosolids (wet waste). With less material
available for blending, individual landfills have reduced the amount of wet waste they can
accept. In addition, Per- and Polyfluoroalkyl Substances (PFAS) have been identified in the
biosolids, creating new challenges for handling, logistics, and disposal. Landfill operators must
now determine which facilities can accept loads containing PFAS and must manage PFAS
concentrations in landfill leachate to comply with local wastewater treatment plant discharge
requirements.

As a result, biosolids must often be transported over longer distances and to multiple landfills to
meet these limitations. The primary cost driver in this contract is the stabilization of wet waste so
it can be reclassified as dry waste for disposal. Stabilization increases the amount of material a
landfill can accept and reduces the environmental impact of PFAS. The biosolids will be mixed
with stabilizing agents such as fly ash or Portland cement, producing a more manageable
material with reduced PFAS leaching potential.
