RECOMMENDATION:

It is recommended the City Commission adopt a Resolution approving the Act 381 Brownfield
Plan for the Zone 32 Phase II project located at 825 Porter and 810 N. Pitcher Streets.

BACKGROUND:

The Brownfield Redevelopment Authority (“BRA”) received a request to consider an Act 381
Brownfield Plan for the Zone 32 Phase II project located at 825 Porter and 810 N. Pitcher Streets
in Kalamazoo, Michigan.

The proposed redevelopment involves approximately 0.57 acres sold to Zone 32 Phase Two,
LLC ("Developer") by the BRA in June 2026. The project will include construction of a new
mixed-use residential development consisting of thirty-eight (38) residential rental units and
approximately 325 square feet of ground-floor commercial space. The residential unit mix is
anticipated to include ten (10) studio apartments, eighteen (18) one-bedroom apartments, and ten
(10) two-bedroom apartments.

The project proposes ten (10) income-restricted studio units, including two (2) units affordable to
households earning approximately forty percent (40%) of Area Median Income (“AMI”) and
eight (8) units affordable to households earning approximately sixty percent (60%) of AMI. The
affordable units are proposed to remain income restricted for a period of thirteen (13) years,
representing approximately twenty-six percent (26%) of the total residential units within the
development.

Construction is anticipated to begin during summer 2026 with project completion anticipated in
fall 2027. The estimated total private investment is approximately $11 million.

The Brownfield Plan requests reimbursement of eligible activities under Act 381, including
environmental assessment activities, infrastructure and safety improvements, site preparation
activities, plan and work plan preparation and implementation costs, contingency costs, and
financing gap costs associated with the project’s affordable housing component. The total
reimbursement request is estimated at $2,028,406.

The eligible activities proposed within the Brownfield Plan include:

• Phase I Environmental Site Assessment: $3,000
• Infrastructure and Safety Improvements: $420,044
• Site Preparation Activities: $270,500
• Financing Gap Activities: $1,151,280
• Brownfield Plan/Work Plan Preparation: $30,000
• Brownfield Plan/Work Plan Implementation: $50,000
• 15% Contingency: $103,582

The project is also anticipated to request a Neighborhood Enterprise Zone (“NEZ”) tax
abatement for a period of up to fifteen (15) years.

Based on current projections, the redevelopment is anticipated to increase the taxable value of
the property from approximately $0 to $1.7 million after construction. The Brownfield Plan
anticipates tax increment capture for a period of up to thirty (30) years, including approximately
twenty-five (25) years of developer reimbursement followed by five (5) years of capture
dedicated to the local Brownfield Revolving Fund. The project is estimated to generate
approximately $566,880 in local Brownfield Revolving Fund revenue and approximately
$199,659 in local-only administrative fees over the life of the Brownfield Plan.

City staff and the City's third-party financial consultant, Plante Moran, reviewed the project as
part of the underwriting process. The review determined that, absent public assistance, the
project would not be financially feasible and would be unlikely to proceed as proposed. The
underwriting further concluded that the project becomes financially feasible with the
combination of private investment and public support provided through City, State, County, and
philanthropic funding sources.

Sub-Goal:
Economic Assistance - Use investments, incentives, and policies to reach community-wide
goals for affordable housing, increase public transportation, local business success, and to avoid
involuntary displacement.

Zone 32 Phase II will use Brownfield TIF as an economic assistance tool to support private
investment by leveraging bank financing, helping make the project financially feasible. In
addition, the Housing TIF provision under Act 381 provides support for units with below-market
rents, offsetting the difference between what the project can charge and the rent needed to make
the building financially viable and units affordable as workforce housing. This project is
committing 10 units at rents affordable to households at or below 60% of Area Median Income,
ensuring these units remain accessible while supporting overall project stability.

COMMUNITY ENGAGEMENT:

Consult (two-way conversation) – the community will have a chance to react to the project
through two-way conversation.

Brownfield projects are required to comply with public notice and hearing requirements
established under Public Act 381 of the State of Michigan.

Consistent with the City’s Public Participation Plan, this project was evaluated as having a “high
impact, small geography” profile, for which a “two-way conversation” level of engagement is
considered appropriate. This engagement level is intended to ensure that directly affected
stakeholders are informed and provided meaningful opportunity to participate.

In alignment with these expectations, the BRA conducted outreach to impacted taxing
jurisdictions through direct mailing related to the proposed tax increment capture. Additionally, a
publicly noticed meeting and hearing were held to provide an opportunity for stakeholder input
prior to BRA consideration of the Brownfield Plan.

FISCAL IMPACT:

The Brownfield Plan is anticipated to generate approximately $199,659 in local-only
administrative fees to support Brownfield Redevelopment Authority program administration and
eligible operational activities over the life of the tax increment capture period. The Plan is also
anticipated to generate approximately $566,880 in revenue to the local Brownfield Revolving
Fund following completion of developer reimbursement obligations.
