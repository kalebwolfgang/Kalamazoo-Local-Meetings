RECOMMENDATION:

It is recommended that the City Commission approve an agreement extension to Local Initiatives
Support Corporation for the 2023 grant awarded from the Coronavirus State and Local Fiscal
Recovery Funds. This grant was for $1,500,000 to rehabilitate approximately 48 rental units to
increase the number of units available to low-income households with rental assistance.

BACKGROUND:

On August 21, 2023, the City Commission approved a grant to Local Initiatives Support
Corporation (LISC) for a rental rehabilitation program. This program provides funding to local
property owners/landlords for rehabilitation of existing rental units that they will then make
available to households utilizing a rental subsidy. The use of a rental subsidy through a federal or
state agency requires housing quality standards that often result in needed improvements
(additional expense). These funds will help to mitigate the extra expense seen in the acceptance
of rental subsidies. The goal is to create more units available in the City of Kalamazoo for those
needing rental assistance.

LISC is very close to completing this project and meeting the required agreement outcomes of 48
rehabilitated rental units. To date, 41 units are complete and three are under contract and close to
completion. The remaining four required funds to be reprogrammed due to difficulties with the
necessary funding time constraints. The requested extension through September 30, 2026 will
provide LISC and the remaining property owners with the needed time to complete the
rehabilitation and rent to low-income tenants.

STRATEGIC VISION ALIGNMENT:

This agreement extension continues to support the following strategic vision goals as it results in
affordable housing:

    1. Economic Vitality: Promote the revitalization and redevelopment of existing properties to
       encourage sustainable economic growth.
    2. Safe Community: Proactively reinvest in neighborhoods through support of programs to
       help residents fix, rehabilitate, and stabilize homes through critical home repair
       assistance, education programs, and weatherization.
    3. Shared Prosperity: Coordinate services to address basic needs and well-being, including
       transportation, housing, childcare, and financial capability.

COMMUNITY ENGAGEMENT:

All potential projects funded through SLFRF dollars were evaluated against the five main City
Commission and Community Priorities. To create these priorities, the following public outreach
resources were utilized:

     • Community Survey (statistically significant) conducted every two years, with the last
       completed in 2021
     • ARPA/Stimulus Survey, which was issued in June of 2021 and received 1,500 responses
     • 30-day public comment period on the ARPA funding
     • Feedback from the City Commission during their annual retreats as to priorities
     • Imagine Kalamazoo 2025 and the engagement received in its creation

These sources were reviewed, and common themes discovered, which were brought back to the
City Commission for approval prior to allocation of SLFRF funds. The maintenance of quality
affordable housing as a priority was a result of this public outreach.

In addition, the original agreement was reviewed and approved at a regular meeting of the City
Commission on August 21, 2023 as a regular agenda item, allowing for community input.

FISCAL IMPACT:

No fiscal impact; all funds have been fully expended by the City. This is a deadline extension
only.
