RECOMMENDATION:

It is recommended that the City Commission adopt a resolution establishing the B on Burdick
NEZ District proposed at the southwest corner of Vine Street & South Burdick Street.

BACKGROUND:

The Neighborhood Enterprise Zone (NEZ) Act (Act 147 of 1992, as amended) (“Act”) provides
a tax incentive for a defined period of time for new construction or substantial rehabilitation of
residential structures. The stated purpose of the legislation is to: improve the housing stock in
distressed areas where little or no new construction is occurring and where housing is in need of
repair. The legislation is intended to spur residential investment where it might not otherwise
occur. Changes in recent years have expanded the types of NEZ's and projects which would
qualify for an NEZ. This NEZ district would be in support of a five-story mixed-use building
called the B on Burdick.

This will be the first single-project NEZ in the City of Kalamazoo, where the proposed boundary
is the extent of the B on Burdick mixed use development, which is currently under site plan
review. The project features 85 residential units in a mixed-use building with ground floor retail
grocery, gym and a daycare. The current development site is made up of twelve (12) existing
parcels which have a mixture of residential structures and commercial property. All the existing
structures would be removed from these parcels for the proposed single-building mixed-use
structure.

The proposed development by Bogan Developments, in partnership with others, including
Bronson Hospitals, would be in support of Bronsons collaborative plan to develop and encourage
uses and housing in support of a healthy lifestyle campus. The NEZ and development would
support housing for the community, including Bronson Hospital as a major employer in the

A NEZ district would support the project by providing a reduction in property taxes for 9 to 15
years through the approval of a Certificate of tax reduction which can be applied for, after a NEZ
district is created. A Certificate would require approval from the City Commission after it is
applied for. Depending on affordability, surface parking and other options, a project can qualify
for up to 15 years of tax exemption. In January, the development received housing funds from
Kalamazoo County and an Employer Assisted Housing (EAH) grant through the Michigan
Economic Development Corporation (MEDC). A NEZ district would provide an opportunity to
support this mixed-use development with the rise in construction costs, due-diligence and
inflation impacts. The City is working with the developer on this NEZ and the City Brownfield
Redevelopment Authority for a Brownfield Plan.

In 2025, as part of the early due diligence for this property development, the applicant rezoned
properties to D3 zone district, where the entire parcel will be zoned pending approval of the
parcel combination. The application to combine the parcels has been submitted for approval. A
portion of the property is in the Vine Historic District and received Historic District Commission
(HDC) approval to remove the buildings at their April 15th 2025 meeting and approval for the
building to be built on site has also received HDC approval.

The resolution to create the NEZ District will be heard at the March 2nd City Commission
meeting. The two meetings, including this public hearing, are requirements of the State law to
initiate the process to create a district. As the development advances through due diligence and
various City review processes, the request moves forward now to align the request with a strait
of construction date later this year. An application for an NEZ certificate is provided before a
building permit is issued. The applicant would come to the commission to request an NEZ
certificate later this year before construction of the building is underway.

FISCAL IMPACT:

Projects within a NEZ District with approved certificates will receive a tax abatement of
approximately half of their full tax obligation for a period of between 6 and 15 years, based on
what the project is bringing forward.

The impact on the City in terms of staff time is:
i. Staff time will be needed for additional assessing and treasury paperwork associated with the
NEZ certificates and applications. The current project would require the processing of one
certificate.
ii. Staff time will be needed for housing and building inspections. Each inspection and
application process has application and cost recovery fees which will offset the impact on the
city's budget.
iii. Staff time will be needed for annual monitoring of the terms of the project for which it
received a certificate for tax abatement, such as for unit affordability.
iv. This proposed NEZ will be accomplished with existing staffing.
