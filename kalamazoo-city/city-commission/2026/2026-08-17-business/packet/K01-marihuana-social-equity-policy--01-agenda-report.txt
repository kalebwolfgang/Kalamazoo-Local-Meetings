SUBJECT:       Adoption of an Amended Marihuana Social Equity Policy

RECOMMENDATION:

It is recommended that the City Commission adopt the amended Marihuana Social Equity
Policy.

BACKGROUND:

On May 18, 2020, the City Commission adopted the City's Marihuana Social Equity Policy in
conjunction with local zoning and licensing regulations authorizing adult-use marihuana
businesses within the City of Kalamazoo. The policy was subsequently amended on June 1,
2020, following updates to the State of Michigan's Marihuana Regulatory Agency Social Equity
Program.

The original policy was designed to reduce barriers to participation in the regulated marihuana
industry for individuals and communities disproportionately impacted by prior marihuana
prohibition and enforcement. The policy established social equity incentives, including
marihuana business permit fee reductions and zoning separation distance exceptions for
qualifying individuals and business entities. The policy also dedicated a portion of local
marihuana permit fee revenue and State marihuana excise tax revenue toward social equity
programming.

Since adoption of the policy in 2020, the City has gained implementation experience, and the
regulated marihuana industry has changed significantly. Changes in market conditions, increased
competition, and evolving economic realities have created an opportunity to evaluate how the
City's social equity investments can best advance long-term economic opportunity.

The proposed amendments preserve the core components of the existing policy, including social
equity eligibility requirements, permit fee reductions, zoning incentives, and support for licensed
marihuana businesses. The amendments update the use of Social Equity Fund resources by

The amended policy organizes eligible uses of Social Equity Fund resources around four priority
areas:

- Access to Capital and Asset Acquisition: Supporting efforts that help residents acquire
  appreciating assets, including homeownership, business ownership, and commercial property.

- Ownership Retention and Growth: Supporting efforts that help residents maintain
  ownership, stabilize assets, expand businesses, and increase economic value.

- Asset Protection and Transfer: Supporting legal and technical assistance that protects
  resident-owned assets and facilitates intergenerational transfer.

- Cannabis Business Retention and Education: Continuing support for licensed marihuana
  businesses through education, regulatory information, networking, and industry support.

The proposed amendments align the City's Social Equity Policy with broader economic
development goals focused on entrepreneurship, ownership, community wealth building, and
ensuring residents benefit from economic growth.
