PURPOSE:

The purpose of this policy is to address the economic impacts of marihuana prohibition and
enforcement by expanding access to ownership, capital, and economic opportunity for residents
and communities disproportionately impacted by prior marihuana enforcement. This policy
applies to all marihuana businesses permitted to operate within the City of Kalamazoo.

SCOPE:

This policy provides social equity incentives for qualifying marihuana businesses and defines the
eligible uses of the social equity allocation of local marihuana permit fee revenue and State
marihuana excise tax revenue received by the City.

Social equity funding shall be used to support programs, services, and initiatives that produce
measurable wealth-building outcomes for residents of the Northside, Eastside, and Edison
neighborhoods. Funded activities will focus on expanding access to ownership, supporting the
retention and growth of resident-owned assets, facilitating the protection and transfer of assets,
and supporting the long-term viability of licensed marihuana businesses operating within the City
of Kalamazoo.

POLICY:

A.      Fee Reductions

        The City marihuana business permit fee required of qualifying social equity individuals and
        business entities is reduced as follows:

        1) A 25% reduction in the City permit fee for a sole proprietor who is a current resident,
           or a business entity composed of majority (51% or more) ownership by one or more
           persons who are current residents, of City Census Tracts 1 (Eastside), 9, 10, (Edison),
           and/or 2.02, 3 (Northside) and who have been residents of these Census Tracts for
           the 3 years prior to applying for a marihuana business permit.

        2) A reduction in the City permit fee for one, but not both, of the following:

           •     A 25% reduction in the City permit fee for a current City resident or group of City
                 residents with a marihuana-related misdemeanor conviction or expunged
                 conviction that does not involve distribution of a controlled substance to a minor,
                 who have 51% or more ownership interest in the business, and who has or have
                 resided in the City for the 3 years prior to applying for a marihuana business permit.

B.      Zoning Code Exceptions

        1) A business entity operating as a marihuana retailer may locate 500 feet from another
           marihuana retailer in conformity with the City zoning code if:

           •     The applicant, or a majority (51% or more) of the applicant-owners is a current City
                 resident within City Census Tracts 1 (Eastside), 9, 10 (Edison), and/or 2.02, 3
                 (Northside) and have been residents of these Census Tracts for the 3 years prior
                 to applying for a marihuana business permit; or

           •     A current City resident or group of City residents with a marihuana-related
                 misdemeanor or felony conviction, that does not involve delivery or distribution of
                 a controlled substance to a minor, and who has 51% or more ownership interest
                 in the business.

        2) A business entity operating as a marihuana microbusiness or class A microbusiness
           may locate 250 feet from another microbusiness or class A microbusiness in
           conformity with the City zoning code if:

           •     An applicant, or a majority (51% or more) of the applicant-owners is a current City
                 resident within City Census Tracts 1 (Eastside), 9, 10 (Edison), and/or 2.02, 3
                 (Northside) and have been residents of these Census Tracts for the past 3 years
                 prior to applying for a marihuana business permit; or

           •     A current City resident or group of City residents with a marihuana-related
                 misdemeanor or felony conviction or expunged marihuana-related conviction that
                 does not involve delivery or distribution of a controlled substance to a minor, and
                 who has 51% or more ownership interest in the business.

C.      Use of Funds

        1) At least twenty-five (25%) of local marihuana business permit fee revenue and State
           marihuana excise tax revenue received by the City shall be allocated to programs,
           services, and initiatives. No more than fifteen percent (15%) of the total annual Social
           Equity Fund allocation may be used for administrative costs.

           Funded activities shall be aligned with the following pillars:

           a. Access to Capital and Asset Acquisition – up to 35%

               Funds may support programs and initiatives that assist eligible residents in
               acquiring ownership of appreciating assets.

               Eligible activities may include, but are not limited to:

               •   Down payment and closing cost assistance for owner-occupied housing
               •   Startup and expansion capital for new businesses
               •   Commercial property acquisition assistance
               •   Equipment, tools, and infrastructure necessary to establish a business
               •   Gap financing and other capital access programs that facilitate business or
                   asset acquisition

           b. Ownership Retention and Growth – up to 30%

               Funds may support programs and initiatives that assist eligible residents in
               retaining ownership of appreciating assets and increasing the value, productivity,
               or income generated by those assets.

               Eligible activities may include, but are not limited to:

               •   Housing stabilization assistance intended to prevent the loss of owner-
                   occupied housing
               •   Business stabilization assistance for resident-owned businesses
               •   Programs that connect resident-owned businesses to contracting,
                   procurement, and market opportunities
               •   Assistance that supports business expansion, revenue generation, or
                   increased utilization of resident-owned assets
               •   Investments that preserve ownership and support the continued productive use
                   of resident-owned assets

           c. Asset Protection and Transfer – up to 10%

               •   Estate planning services

               •   Community outreach and education on cannabis health and safety
               •   Cannabis state and federal policy education
               •   Educational programming for cannabis business owners and operators
               •   Information sharing related to regulatory changes and industry developments
               •   Cannabis industry networking and peer learning opportunities
               •   Industry meetings, workshops, and events that support knowledge sharing and
                   collaboration among cannabis businesses
               •   Tuition reimbursement for KVCC cannabis-related courses
               •   Expungement clinics/assistance for individuals with prior marihuana
                   convictions

           eligibility at time of City permit renewal.

HISTORY:

May 18, 2020           This policy was initially adopted by the City Commission in conjunction with
                       zoning and licensing ordinances authorizing the establishment of adult-use
                       marihuana businesses.

June 1, 2020           The day after the City Commission first adopted this policy, the Michigan
                       Marijuana Regulatory Agency announced changes to its Social Equity
                       Program, effective June 1, 2020. As a result, the City Commission amended
                       this policy to incorporate a 40% reduction in permit fees for a marihuana
                       felony conviction not involving delivery or distribution to a minor.
