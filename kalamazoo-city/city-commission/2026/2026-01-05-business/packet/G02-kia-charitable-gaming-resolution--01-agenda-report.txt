SUBJECT:        Resolution of Non-Profit Status for Charitable Gaming License - Kalamazoo

RECOMMENDATION:

It is recommended that the City Commission adopt a resolution recognizing the Kalamazoo
Institute of Arts as a non-profit organization operating in the community for the purpose of
obtaining a charitable gaming license.

BACKGROUND:

Founded in 1924, the Kalamazoo Institute of Arts (KIA) is a non-profit organization and a
cornerstone of the arts and culture community in Kalamazoo. The KIA holds a permanent
collection of nearly 5,000 works of art, presents touring and collection exhibitions in their 10
galleries; offers four terms of art classes at the Kirk Newman Art School along with other
educational programming; and houses the KIA Gallery Shop, which features artwork by local
and international artisans.

On March 20, 2026, the KIA would like to hold a large, in-house raffle at their event, Future:
REMIX. This is a ticketed event inspired by their upcoming exhibition, Future Now: Virtual
Sneakers to Cutting Edge Kicks. The event will feature DJs, local food and beverages, and all
galleries will be open for guests to explore. Guests in attendance will be eligible to purchase
raffle tickets and winners will be announced during the event. A minimum of three winners will
be drawn with the top prize being a voucher for an art class at the Kirk Newman Arts School
with a value of up to $380.00. All funds raised from the raffle will be put towards the KIA’s
general operating fund which makes it possible for the institution to live its belief that the arts are
for everyone, ensuring a robust slate of free and accessible programming for the Kalamazoo
community.

In order to conduct the raffle, the KIA must obtain a charitable gaming license through the
Michigan Lottery Bureau. As part of the licensing process, the state requires the local unit of
government to establish and verify the applicant’s non-profit status through the adoption of a
Local Governing Body Resolution for Charitable Gaming Licenses. The City Clerk has received

Discussion
In his role as the Clerk of the City Commission, the City Clerk receives requests from non-profit
organizations who want to apply for charitable gaming licenses. The Clerk reviews the requests,
verifies the organizations' non-profit status, and presents qualified organizations to the City
Commission for recognition as "non-profit organizations operating in the community."
