Imagine Kalamazoo 2035 Design It! Continues
The Design It! phase of Imagine Kalamazoo 2035 continues with upcoming neighborhood meetings in
Oakwood, Parkview Hills/Colony Farms/Hill n Brook, Northside, and West Main Hill over the few weeks. These

the environment, neighborhoods, and asset mapping. These online tools mirror the meeting activities and allow
residents to contribute at their convenience. You can find them at www.kalamazoocity.org/IK2035.

New Downtown Brand Unveiled
A new brand for downtown Kalamazoo was unveiled last week at an event held at the Xperience. The
updated identity reflects downtown’s energy, creativity, and economic vitality. Residents and visitors will begin
seeing the new branding elements on banners, refuse bins, and other streetscape features throughout the
downtown area.

Water Main Breaks
We have experienced several water main breaks in recent weeks, which is typical for this time of year.
Repeated freezing and thawing cycles cause the ground to expand and contract, placing additional stress on
underground pipes, particularly older cast iron mains, making breaks more likely during winter months. To date,
we have experienced fewer breaks than during the same period last year.

Our Public Services team is responding promptly to each incident to restore service as safely and efficiently
as possible. When repairs require depressurizing a section of the system, Boil Water Advisories are issued as a
precaution while required water quality testing is completed. Crews isolate the smallest feasible section of pipe
before beginning repairs to minimize service disruptions for residents and businesses.

Long term, we continue investing in systematic water main replacement, upgrading aging infrastructure with
more durable materials, and using asset management data to proactively prioritize high-risk areas. These
investments are strengthening system reliability and reducing the likelihood of future breaks. We appreciate the
community’s patience as crews work around the clock to maintain safe, reliable water service.

Bulk Trash Pickup in March
Residents are reminded that bulk trash pickup will take place in the month of March. Residents can place large
items like old furniture, mattresses, etc. out for picked on their collection day. The schedule and complete
guidelines are available in the View from the Curb newsletter, which is mailed to every household twice per
year, and online at www.kalamazoocity.org/BulkTrash.

NFP Review Board Vacancy
The NFP Review Board is seeking applicants to fill a current vacancy. Residents with an interest in smart,
sustainable development are encouraged to apply. Please help spread the word to community members
who may be interested in serving. Applications for all boards and commissions are accepted on a rolling basis,
please encourage your contacts with an interest in serving to apply.

Historic Preservation Commission Awards
May is Historic Preservation Month, and the Historic Preservation Commission will again recognize outstanding
preservation efforts through its Awards of Merit program. Nominations open in March. Past award recipients
have included projects such as the preservation of the State Theatre, notable residential restoration efforts, and
publications preserving Kalamazoo’s history. More information will be shared soon seeking nominations and
regarding eligibility and nomination deadlines.

Rain Barrel Sale
Our partners in Battle Creek are currently hosting this season’s regional Rain Barrel Sale. Using a rain barrel helps
reduce storm water runoff, protect local waterways, and provide a sustainable source of free water for gardens
and landscaping. Orders are open through March 31, with pickup scheduled for April 15. Battle Creek and
Kalamazoo alternate hosting this annual sale as part of our shared commitment to watershed protection and
environmental stewardship. Residents can find information on the sale at www.bcwater.org/events/

Recycling Drop-Off
Staff joined a team of volunteers to host the Recycling Drop-Off event over the weekend at Mayors’ Riverfront
Park. These events provide residents with a responsible disposal option for materials that can’t be recycled
curbside, including EPS foam, dental plastic, electronics, documents for shredding, and large pieces of
cardboard. Events like this support our waste reduction and sustainability goals.
www.kalamazoocity.org/RecyclingDropoff

Demolition Underway at 322 Stockbridge
Demolition is underway at 322 E Stockbridge Avenue, marking the next step in preparing this nearly 15-acre site
in the Edison neighborhood for future redevelopment. The site was acquired using FFE funds, and its size, central
location, and proximity to parks, the Farmers Market, Washington Square, Family Health Center, and major
employers make it a strong opportunity to expand much-needed housing. The vision is to create a walkable,
inclusive neighborhood that reconnects to the existing street grid, offers a mix of housing types and price points,
incorporates green space and storm water infrastructure, and reflects the character of Edison. This direction is
grounded in the Edison Neighborhood Plan, the Kalamazoo County Housing Plan, and Imagine Kalamazoo.
Community engagement will begin soon as planning progresses, with opportunities for residents to help shape
the next phase of the project. More information on this project is available at
www.kalamazoocity.org/322Stockbridge.

KDPS Publishes FAQ on Flock ALPR Cameras
The Kalamazoo Department of Public Safety (KDPS) has published a detailed FAQ regarding its use of Flock
Automated License Plate Reader (ALPR) cameras. We recognize that members of the community have
questions and concerns about privacy and how these tools are used. The FAQ is intended to provide clear
information about safeguards, permitted uses, and accountability measures.

ALPR cameras capture license plates and vehicles visible from public spaces only. The system collects an
image, date, time, and general location. Data itself is not personally identifiable information. KDPS retains 100%
control of its data. Information is shared only with approved Southwest Michigan law enforcement partners. It
is not shared with ICE, not placed on a nationwide search platform, and cannot be sold by the vendor. ALPR
data may not be used for immigration enforcement or investigations involving individuals seeking reproductive
or other health services.

ALPR data has played a critical role in solving serious crimes locally, including homicide investigations, gun
violence cases, child abductions, human trafficking, drug overdose investigations, auto theft recovery, and
kidnapping response. Since implementation, homicides, firearm assaults, shots fired incidents, property crime,
and motor vehicle theft have all declined significantly compared to five years ago. We encourage anyone
interested to review the full FAQ for complete details on policy, safeguards, and oversight:
www.kalamazoopublicsafety.org/FlockFAQ.

KDPS Armed Robbery Case Featured on ID’s Body Cam
Last week, Kalamazoo Department of Public Safety was featured on Investigation Discovery’s Body Cam,
highlighting a 2022 armed robbery investigation that demonstrated the dangerous and unpredictable
situations our officers, investigators, and detectives face in the line of duty.

The episode is now available on demand on HBO Max. (Season 10, Episode 10)

The case involved a violent armed robbery at Tienda Guatemalteca, during which the suspect fired multiple

The suspect has since been sentenced to 17–40 years in prison, with additional time for felony firearm charges.
This case reflects the professionalism, dedication, and commitment of our personnel working to keep the
community safe.

Water Treatment Upgrade Will Start in March
The City of Kalamazoo is implementing a treatment technique change to further strengthen corrosion control in
our public drinking water system. After nearly a decade of study and testing in coordination with the Michigan
Department of Environment, Great Lakes, and Energy (EGLE), the City will transition to a more optimized
corrosion control approach tailored to Kalamazoo’s groundwater chemistry. The update shifts primary
treatment from polyphosphate to orthophosphate, which is more effective at forming a protective lining inside
pipes and reducing the potential for metals, particularly lead, to leach into drinking water.

The change will be rolled out in phases starting in March after upgrades to all 13 water treatment plants,
including modernized phosphate storage, injection, and automation systems. This $13 million investment
is funded through the water utility and supports long-term system reliability and public health protection.
Optimized corrosion control provides an added layer of protection as the City continues replacing all lead
service lines throughout the system.

Additional information for water customers will be shared soon.
