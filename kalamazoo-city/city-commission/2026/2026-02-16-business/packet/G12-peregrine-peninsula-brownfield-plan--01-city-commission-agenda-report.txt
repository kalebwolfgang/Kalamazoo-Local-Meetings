SUBJECT:       Adoption of a Resolution Approving the Act 381 Brownfield Plan for Peregrine
               Peninsula located at 111 Portage Street.

RECOMMENDATION:

It is recommended the City Commission approve the Act 381 Brownfield Plan for Peregrine
Peninsula located at 111 Portage Street.

BACKGROUND:

Following the public hearing held on January 15, 2026, at a meeting of the Brownfield
Redevelopment Authority (BRA), the City Commission was asked to consider adoption of a
resolution approving implementation of the Act 381 Brownfield Plan for Peregrine Peninsula at
111 Portage Street.

At its public hearing, the BRA determined that the Brownfield Plan satisfies the requirements of
Act 381, that the proposed eligible activities and associated costs are reasonable and necessary,
that the proposed financing structure is feasible, and that the amount of captured taxable value is
appropriate. The BRA subsequently approved a resolution recommending implementation of the
Plan and forwarded it to the City Commission for final consideration.

Implementation of the Plan also relies on tax increment revenue participation from the
Downtown Economic Growth Authority (DEGA). On December 15, 2025, the DEGA approved
a 30-year step aside of its tax increment revenues in support of the project. The accompanying
resolution authorizes execution of an interlocal agreement formalizing this commitment and
allowing the BRA to capture tax increment revenues within the DEGA district for the duration of
the Brownfield Plan. This participation is necessary to support reimbursement of eligible
activities identified in the Plan.

Project & Brownfield Plan Overview
The proposed project involves the redevelopment of a single parcel (0.137 acres) located at 111
Portage Street in the City of Kalamazoo. A fire at the property in August 2024 rendered the
second and third floors unusable, primarily due to water damage from fire suppression efforts
that contained the blaze. Before the fire, the second floor was occupied by office space, and the
third floor contained three large residential units.

As part of the building’s rehabilitation, the developer proposes reconfiguring the upper floors to
increase residential housing supply. The third floor will be redesigned to accommodate five
smaller residential units in place of the previous three units, and the second floor will be
converted from office space to six residential apartment units.

The completed rehabilitation will provide a total of 11 residential rental units, primarily one-
bedroom, one-bathroom layouts. Four units will include a den, and two units will be ADA
compliant. The project includes three income-restricted one-bedroom units in accordance with a
Michigan Neighborhoods Award commitment at 60 percent of Area Median Income (AMI), as
well as two additional units meeting requirements associated with the City of Kalamazoo
Neighborhood Enterprise Zone policy. Unit sizes are anticipated to range from approximately
500 to 1,010 square feet.

Policy Alignment
The project aligns with City housing and downtown vitality goals that support the creation of
workforce housing serving households earning approximately 80 to 120 percent of AMI. All
units within the project fall within this target range, which is significant in the downtown market
where rents can exceed these levels. The inclusion of units affordable at 60 percent AMI
provides an uncommon level of affordability for this location and contributed to the BRA,
DEGA, and staff recommendation for support.

In addition to advancing housing affordability objectives, the net increase in residential units
contributes to broader City goals of expanding housing supply, reducing market pressure on
existing housing stock, and supporting long-term housing cost stabilization. Increased residential
density in the downtown core is also anticipated to strengthen pedestrian activity and support
surrounding businesses.

STRATEGIC VISION ALIGNMENT:

Economic Vitality - A supportive infrastructure for growing businesses and stabilizing the local
economy for the benefit of all.

This item advances the Economic Assistance strategic priority by using incentives to expand
housing stock and support housing affordability goals.

COMMUNITY ENGAGEMENT:

Consult (two-way conversation) – the community will have a chance to react to the project
through two-way conversation.

Pursuant to the City’s Public Participation Policy, Brownfield projects are not subject to a
prescribed engagement process under the policy framework. However, these projects are
required to comply with public notice and hearing requirements established under Act 381 of the
State of Michigan.

Consistent with the City’s Public Participation Plan, this project was evaluated as having a “high
impact, small geography” profile, for which a “two way conversation” level of engagement is
considered appropriate. This engagement level is intended to ensure that directly affected
stakeholders are informed and provided meaningful opportunity to participate.
