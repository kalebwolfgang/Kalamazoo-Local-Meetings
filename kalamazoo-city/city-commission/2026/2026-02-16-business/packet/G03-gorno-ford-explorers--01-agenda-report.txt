BACKGROUND:

Fully marked Public Safety vehicles are essential to the Kalamazoo Department of Public
Safety’s (KDPS) ability to provide safe, timely, and effective police, fire, and medical
emergency response. The department maintains a fleet of approximately fifty-six (56) marked
units to support these operations. Due to the high-mileage nature of public safety work and the
significant maintenance costs associated with aging vehicles, several front-line units must be
replaced each year to maintain operational readiness and cost-effective fleet performance.

Public Safety Officers carry an extensive array of gear and specialized equipment in their
assigned vehicles. As a result, many pursuit-rated sedans and smaller SUVs do not meet the
space, load-bearing, and configuration requirements necessary for KDPS operations. The Ford
Police Interceptor Utility (Explorer) platform continues to be one of the most suitable and
reliable options for housing emergency equipment, supporting integrated technology systems,
and sustaining the heavy-duty use associated with public safety functions.

The proposed purchase of three (3) Ford Explorer Police Interceptor Utility vehicles will replace
existing front-line units that have reached the end of their optimal service life. Once the new
vehicles are deployed, the outgoing units will either be rotated to secondary functions within the
department or retired from service, depending on their condition. Front-line public safety
vehicles are operated continuously—often by multiple officers over a 24-hour period, seven days
a week. This operational tempo results in accelerated wear on drive trains, suspension systems,
engines, and interior components. Regular replacement of high-use units is therefore necessary to

FISCAL IMPACT:

The best pricing for the proposed vehicle purchase was the MiDeal Purchasing Program. The
State of Michigan drafted bid specifications for the manufacture of vehicles used by law
enforcement departments, and in turn, received bids from interested automobile companies.
Funds for the purchase of the three (3) proposed Public Safety fleet vehicles are included in the
adopted 2026 General fund Budget in Public Safety capital outlay.
