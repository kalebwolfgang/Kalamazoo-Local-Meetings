RECOMMENDATION:

It is recommended that the City Commission adopt a resolution establishing the Zone 32 - NEZ
District at 825 Porter Street & 810 N Pitcher Street at the June 15th, 2026 City Commission
meeting.

BACKGROUND:

The Neighborhood Enterprise Zone (NEZ) Act (Act 147 of 1992, as amended) (“Act”) provides
a temporary tax reduction for a defined period of time for new construction or substantial
rehabilitation of residential structures in an NEZ district. The stated purpose of the legislation is
to improve the housing stock in distressed areas where little or no new construction is occurring
and where housing is in need of repair. The legislation is intended to spur residential investment
where it might not otherwise occur. Bogan Assett Management is seeking to create an NEZ
district in support of a proposed phase II of Zone 32 phase I, which is a mixed use development
with a child care facility across Pitcher Street. The mixed-use building includes thirty-eight (38)
residential units and a small ground-floor retail space; any certificate request would be based on
the eligible residential improvements.

Within this decade the State has expanded the types of NEZs and types of qualifying residential
developments which qualify for a certificate, which is the formal approval of the reduced taxes
for terms for up to 15 years. The intent of these expansions of qualifying residential and mixed
uses were to promote development of more residential units and varied housing types (e.g house,
duplex, apartments, mixed-use). In order for a project to take advantage of the tax benefits of this
tool, an NEZ district must first be established by the City. Once the boundaries of the district are
approved, individual projects may then request approval of the tax incentives for their project
within the boundaries. The proposed Zone 32 Phase II development would be located within the
proposed New NEZ district and may be eligible to apply for a certificate if all state and local

A third-party financial review completed as part of the Brownfield Plan evaluation included a

The type of NEZ being proposed allows for new construction dwelling units. The goals for
establishing this district, to be known as Zone 32 NEZ, are to:

1. Promote the creation of new residential uses;
2. Utilize vacant and underutilized properties;
3. Promote development in areas where housing is needed;
4. Promote development where existing infrastructure and amenities exist;
5. Advance the City's vision for attainable housing, economic development, mixed-income
development, housing type diversity, utilization of public transit, and mobility-focused initiatives
and infill.

The final authority on the decision to grant the NEZ tax reduction is the State of Michigan Tax
Commission, who verifies the local approval of a district by its resolution to create an NEZ
district. After an issuance of a resolution, an applicant then has to apply to the State of Michigan
for approval or denial of a NEZ Certificate. Note that a request for a Certificate will also require
City Commission approval after the creation of any NEZ district. In the case of a new building,
as proposed for this request, if approved by the State Tax Commission, the Certificate for tax
reduction is held by the State until a Certificate of Occupancy is issued and then the years of
reduced taxes start from that point, from the new assessed land value. If this district is created,
staff recommends a future sunset of the district after five (5) years, unless continued district
availability is warranted under the City Commission NEZ Policy.

Staff finds that the proposed Zone 32 NEZ District is consistent with the intent of Public Act 147
of 1992, as amended, and the City's NEZ Policy. The proposed district supports reinvestment in
vacant and underutilized property, advances new housing production, leverages existing
infrastructure, and provides a mechanism for a future certificate request to be reviewed through
the City and State approval process.

FISCAL IMPACT:

Projects within a NEZ District may receive a reduced property tax obligation on eligible
residential improvements, generally estimated at approximately half of the standard tax
obligation during the certificate period, depending on the certificate type and applicable
calculations.

The impact on the City in terms of staff time is:
i. Staff time will be needed for additional assessing and treasury paperwork associated with the
NEZ certificates and applications. The current project would require the processing of one
certificate.
ii. Staff time will be needed for housing and building inspections. Each inspection and
application process has application and cost recovery fees which will offset the impact on the
city's budget.
iii. Staff time will be needed for annual monitoring of the terms of the project for which it
received a certificate for tax abatement, such as for unit affordability.
iv. This proposed NEZ will be accomplished with existing staffing.
