SUBJECT:       Approval of Amendments to the City of Kalamazoo Marihuana Social Equity
               Policy

RECOMMENDATION:

It is recommended that the City Commission approve the amended Marihuana Social Equity
Policy.

The proposed amendments modernize the City’s existing social equity framework by shifting the
policy away from a primary focus on participation in the cannabis industry and toward long-term
community wealth building, ownership, and economic mobility for residents in historically
underinvested neighborhoods. The amended policy establishes a clearer framework for the use of
local marihuana permit fee revenue and State marihuana excise tax revenue to support
measurable outcomes tied to homeownership, business ownership, commercial ownership, asset
retention, and generational wealth transfer.

BACKGROUND:

On May 18, 2020, the City Commission adopted the City’s original Marihuana Social Equity
Policy alongside local zoning and licensing regulations authorizing adult-use marihuana
businesses within the City of Kalamazoo. The policy was subsequently amended on June 1,
2020, following updates to the State of Michigan’s Cannabis Regulatory Agency Social Equity
Program.

The original policy was designed to reduce barriers to participation in the regulated cannabis
industry for individuals and communities disproportionately impacted by prior marihuana
enforcement. Since adoption, staff have continued to evaluate implementation outcomes, broader
cannabis market conditions, and strategies that most effectively support long-term economic
mobility and wealth creation within Kalamazoo neighborhoods.

Over the past several years, the cannabis industry has experienced substantial financial instability
statewide, including significant product price declines, market oversupply, increased taxation,
and ongoing operational challenges.

In evaluating future policy direction, staff reviewed broader economic development and wealth-
building strategies and identified that long-term economic mobility is more strongly tied to
ownership of appreciating assets, including homes, businesses, and commercial property, than
workforce participation alone. Staff also identified continued systemic barriers to ownership,
access to capital, asset retention, and generational wealth transfer within the Northside, Eastside,
and Edison neighborhoods.

The proposed amendments retain the City’s existing social equity incentives related to permit fee
reductions and zoning separation distance reductions for qualifying applicants and permit
holders. In addition, the amendments establish a revised framework for the use of social equity-
related revenues centered around three community wealth-building pillars:

1. First Access to Capital and Asset Acquisition

This pillar supports direct access to ownership opportunities through investments such as down
payment assistance, business startup support, commercial property acquisition assistance,
licensing support, tools, equipment, and other forms of entry-point capital intended to lead
directly to ownership and asset acquisition.

2. Ownership Retention and Growth

This pillar supports long-term stabilization and growth for new owners and entrepreneurs
through activities such as business stabilization assistance, procurement and contract readiness
support, housing stabilization assistance, and other investments intended to help residents
maintain and grow recently acquired assets.

3. Asset Protection and Transfer

This pillar supports the long-term preservation and transfer of wealth through services such as
wills and trusts, title clearing, heirs’ property resolution, succession planning, LLC formation,
and other legal protections that help preserve ownership across generations.

The amended policy prioritizes measurable outcomes related to increased homeownership and
business ownership, stabilization of new owners during early years of ownership, preservation of
resident-owned assets, and creation of transferable generational wealth.

If adopted, the amended policy will serve as the foundation for a future Social Equity Request
for Proposals (RFP) process focused on measurable community wealth-building outcomes
aligned with the City’s Economic Development Strategy and broader equitable economic
development goals.

FISCAL IMPACT:

The amended policy establishes that at least fifty percent (50%) of local marihuana business
permit fee revenue and State marihuana excise tax revenue received by the City shall be
allocated toward programs, services, and initiatives aligned with the policy’s community wealth-
building framework.

No additional General Fund appropriation is required as part of this action.
