CITY OF KALAMAZOO                                                CITY COMMISSION POLICY

Subject: Draft Marihuana Social Equity Policy
Date: May 11, 2026
PURPOSE:
The purpose of this policy is to address the long-term economic impacts of marihuana
prohibition and enforcement by expanding access to ownership, wealth-building opportunities,
and economic participation for residents and communities disproportionately impacted by prior
marihuana enforcement. This policy applies to all marihuana businesses permitted to operate
within the City of Kalamazoo. Current permit holders shall comply with the requirements of this
policy at the time of permit renewal, including submission and supplementation of any required
social equity plan documentation.
SCOPE:
This policy seeks to:
• Allow targeted focus on disproportionally impacted individuals and City neighborhoods
• Reduce barriers to entering the marihuana industry specific to Kalamazoo residents
• Provide funding for programs, services, and initiatives that support wealth-building outcomes
for residents of the Northside, Eastside, and Edison neighborhoods
POLICY:
A. Fee reductions
The City marihuana business permit fee required of qualifying social equity individuals and
business entities is reduced as follows:
1) A 25% reduction in the City permit fee for a sole proprietor who is a resident, or a business
entity composed of majority (51% or more) ownership by one or more persons who are residents,
of City Census Tracts 1 (Eastside), 9, 10, (Edison), and/or 2.02, 3 (Northside) for at least 3 years
prior to applying for a marihuana business permit.
2) A reduction in the City permit fee for one, but not both, of the following:
• A 25% reduction in the City permit fee for a City resident or group of City residents with a
marihuana-related misdemeanor conviction or expunged conviction that does not involve
distribution of a controlled substance to a minor, who have 51% or more ownership interest in
the business, and who has or have resided in the City for at least 3 years prior to applying for a
marihuana business permit.
• A 40% reduction in the City permit fee for a City resident or group of City residents with a
marihuana-related felony conviction or expunged conviction that does not involve delivery or
distribution of a controlled substance to a minor, who have 51% or more ownership interest in

B. Zoning Code Exceptions
1) A business entity operating as a marihuana retailer may locate 500 feet from another
marihuana retailer in conformity with the City zoning code if:
• The applicant, or a majority (51% or more) of the applicant-owners is a City resident within
City Census Tracts 1 (Eastside), 9, 10 (Edison), and/or 2.02, 3 (Northside) for the 3 years prior to
applying for a marihuana business permit; or
• A City resident or group of City residents with a marihuana-related misdemeanor or felony
conviction, that does not involve delivery or distribution of a controlled substance to a minor and
who has 51% or more ownership interest in the business
2) A business entity operating as a marihuana microbusiness or class A microbusiness may
locate 250 feet from another microbusiness or class A microbusiness in conformity with the City
zoning code if:
• An applicant, or a majority (51% or more) of the applicant-owners is a City resident within
City Census Tracts 1 (Eastside), 9, 10 (Edison), and/or 2.02, 3 (Northside) for the past 3 years; or
• A City resident or group of City residents with a marihuana-related misdemeanor or felony
conviction or expunged marihuana-related conviction that does not involve delivery or
distribution of a controlled substance to a minor and who has 51% or more ownership interest in
the business
C. Use of Funds
1) At least fifty percent (50%) of local marihuana business permit fee revenue and State
marihuana excise tax revenue received by the City shall be allocated to programs, services, and
initiatives that support measurable community wealth building outcomes for residents of the
Northside, Eastside, and Edison neighborhoods.
Funded activities shall be aligned with the following pillars:
a. First Access to Capital and Asset Acquisition
Funds may support programs and initiatives that provide residents with direct access to the
capital, financing assistance, or acquisition support necessary to obtain ownership of
appreciating assets.

Eligible activities may include, but are not limited to:
   •   Down payment and closing cost assistance for owner-occupied housing
   •   Startup capital for new businesses, including assistance with licensing, insurance, legal
       formation, inventory, equipment, or operational startup costs
   •   Commercial property acquisition assistance
   •   Capital access programs for entrepreneurs and microenterprises
   •   Equipment, tools, or infrastructure necessary to establish or expand a business
   •   Gap financing programs intended to move residents from business preparation into active
       ownership
Priority shall be given to programs that result in a completed transaction or acquisition, including
homeownership, business ownership, commercial ownership, or other forms of appreciating
asset ownership.
b. Ownership Retention and Growth
Funds may support programs and initiatives that strengthen the long-term viability, stability, and
revenue-generating capacity of resident-owned assets and businesses.
Eligible activities may include, but are not limited to:
   •   Business stabilization and growth assistance
   •   Contract readiness and procurement assistance for resident-owned businesses
   •   Programs connecting resident-owned businesses to contracting opportunities with public,
       institutional, or private sector partners
   •   Technical assistance tied to revenue generation, expansion, or operational sustainability
   •   Housing stabilization assistance intended to prevent the loss of recently acquired owner-
       occupied housing
   •   Programs that preserve ownership during periods of financial hardship or unexpected
       capital need
   •   Investments that support equity growth, business scaling, or long-term asset appreciation
Programs funded under this section should prioritize measurable outcomes related to retained
ownership, revenue generation, business growth, equity preservation, or increased asset value.
c. Asset Protection and Transfer

Funds may support programs and initiatives that protect resident-owned assets and ensure the
legal transfer, preservation, and continuation of ownership across generations.
Eligible activities may include, but are not limited to:
   •   Estate planning services
   •   Wills and trusts
   •   Title clearing and heirs’ property resolution
   •   LLC formation and business entity structuring
   •   Operating agreements and succession planning
   •   Legal assistance related to asset preservation or transfer
   •   Reduced-fee or pro bono legal service partnerships that produce completed legal
       documents and enforceable ownership protections
Priority shall be given to programs that result in completed legal protections, documented
ownership security, or successful transfer planning for resident-owned assets or businesses.
D. Compliance with Social Equity Plan
1) Individuals or entities that have received fee reductions and/or reduced separation distance
opportunities based on this social equity policy must provide proof of continued eligibility at
time of City permit renewal.
a. If eligible, they will receive fee deductions when renewing their permit.
b. If no longer eligible:
i. They will not receive fee deductions
ii. If the individual or entity took advantage of reduced separation distance opportunities, they
would no longer be eligible to operate the business at that location.
3) Marihuana businesses seeking renewal of their City-issued permit must provide answers to the
following questions:
a. How many City residents do you currently employ?
b. How many current employees reside in Census Tracts 1 (Eastside); 9,10 (Edison) and/or 2.02,
3 (Northside)?
c. What is your plan to employ residents of the identified census tracts?
d. How many of your employees have prior marihuana-related convictions or expungements,
excepting a conviction for delivery or distribution to a minor?

HISTORY:
May 18, 2020
This policy was initially adopted by the City Commission in conjunction with zoning and
licensing ordinances authorizing the establishment of adult-use marihuana businesses.
June 1, 2020
The day after the City Commission first adopted this policy, the Michigan Marihuana Regulatory
Agency announced changes to its Social Equity Program, effective June 1, 2020. As a result, the
City Commission amended this policy to incorporate a 40% reduction in permit fees for a
marihuana felony conviction not involving delivery or distribution to a minor.
