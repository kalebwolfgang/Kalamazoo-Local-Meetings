Reports and Legislation

City Manager Hankins reviewed items in the written July 20, 2026 City Manager’s       2026-28-18
Report.                                                                               City Manager’s Report

Policy Items                                                                          Policy Items
Vice Mayor Duncan, seconded by Commissioner Slaby, moved that the City                Call-In Public
Commission direct the City Attorney, in concert with the City Manager, to prepare     Comments
alternative amendments to the rules and procedures concerning public comment.
The alternatives should include options regarding the duration of individual public
comments, opportunities for remote participation through call-in technology, and
the placement of general comments on the meeting agenda, together with any
related procedural changes necessary to implement these options. The City Clerk,
City Manager, and City Attorney should also identify legal and operational
considerations on public engagement associated with each alternative and return
them to the City Commission for a vote.

Discussion followed. Commissioners requested the staff response and
recommendation take into consideration and address the following:

-   the current practices of the City’s other boards, commissions, and committees
    regarding call-in public comments, and the need for consistency between the
    City Commission and these boards
-   a proactive plan to deal with hate speech during public comments while
    upholding First Amendment rights
-   the sections of the agenda where call-in comments would be allowed
-   staffing considerations
-   the length of individual public comments
-   the position of general public comments on the agenda

City Manager Hankins indicated he had enough information and clarity to proceed
with the City Commission’s request, and he suggested Commissioners send him
any additional questions and ideas they might have on the issue.

Discussion re: a         Discussion took place regarding a moratorium on data centers.
Moratorium on Data
Centers                  Vice Mayor Duncan suggested the City explore the legality of a two-year
                         moratorium on data centers. There were no objections.

                         City Attorney Leal indicated he could give a 15-20-minute presentation at a
                         Committee of the Whole meeting that would focus on the definition of, and legal
                         parameters for, moratoriums.

                         Vice Mayor Duncan requested that City Attorney Leal give this presentation at the
                         next Committee of the Whole meeting.

                         Commissioners suggested the following topics for exploration:

                             -   the full range of tools available to help the community
                             -   potential moratoriums on other types of facilities, such as those involving
                                 cryptocurrency, artificial intelligence, and battery storage
                             -   the intersection of this issue with the Imagine Kalamazoo 2035 Master
                                 Plan process
                             -   the zoning tools currently available and the ones potentially available if
                                 the current zoning rules were changed

                         City Attorney Leal requested clarification on the scope of his presentation.

                         City Manager Hankins offered that a moratorium would give space for staff to
                         study the topics Commissioners had mentioned.
