RECITALS

A. On July 1, 2026, Community Action Michigan filed with the City Clerk a citizen-
   initiative petition proposing to amend the Kalamazoo City Charter to establish a
   Citizens’ Healthcare Action Committee (“Citizens’ Action Committee”); to create the
   Healthcare Action Plan and make recommendations to address healthcare costs and
   healthcare inaccessibility; to prescribe the powers and duties of the Citizens’ Action
   Committee; to prescribe the Citizens’ Action Committee’s composition and
   organization; and to require the public overview, and adoption of the Healthcare Action
   Plan.

B. The petition was accompanied with 870 petition sheets containing approximately 5,779
   signatures. Under the Home Rule City Act, MCL 117.25(1), a petition must be signed
   by at least five percent (2,738) of the approximate 54,769 qualified and registered
   electors in the City of Kalamazoo, according to the Qualified Voter File. Community
   Action Michigan believes the petition contains the sufficient number of signatures to
   qualify for placement on the Tuesday, November 3, 2026, general election ballot.

C. If the ballot proposal is adopted by the voters, it will be added to the City Charter under
   new Section 200.

D. The citizen-initiative petition does not contain a ballot question, so the City Attorney
   and Community Action Michigan’s attorney jointly agreed on the language of the ballot
   question contained in this Resolution that conforms with the Home Rule City Act, MCL
   117.21(2).

THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED:

1. The Kalamazoo City Commission proposes that the following citizen-initiative Charter
   amendment be submitted to the electors for adoption or rejection at the general election
   to be held on Tuesday, November 3, 2026:

   SECTION 200. Citizens’ Advisory Healthcare Action Committee

   A. CREATION

   A Citizens’ Advisory Healthcare Action Committee (“Citizens’ Action
   Committee”) is hereby created to prepare a Healthcare Action Plan and make
   recommendations to address prohibitive healthcare costs and healthcare
   inaccessibility in a biennial action plan.

   B. FINDINGS, PURPOSE, INTENT

   The health, safety, and welfare of the residents of the City are served by regular,
   transparent, and public evaluation of healthcare affordability, accessibility,
   insurance and benefit coverage, and medical service availability. A recurring
   process for collecting public testimony, reviewing data, and evaluating policy
   impacts will assist the City and the jurisdiction’s federal and state representatives
   in identifying barriers to healthcare access and in recommending effective
   responses. It is thus the purpose and intent of this Section to establish the Citizens’
   Action Committee that will prepare a biennial Healthcare Action Plan and make
   recommendations to the City Commission and to provide a public process through
   which the public will be heard and needs assessed.

   C. HEALTHCARE ACTION PLAN REQUIRED

   A Healthcare Action Plan shall be developed by the Citizens’ Action Committee
   and presented to the City Commission by October 1 of every even-numbered year.
   At a minimum, the Healthcare Action Plan shall set out:

   (1) How healthcare affordability and accessibility, insurance and benefit coverage,
       and medical service availability have changed for City residents over the prior
       two years based on available population and public health data, hospital and
       other health care provider records, and public testimony;
   (2) What federal, state, and local policy decisions and other factors have
       contributed to changes in healthcare affordability and accessibility, insurance
       and benefit coverage, and service availability;
   (3) How federal and state elected officials who represent the City voted on relevant
       policies, including budgets and legislation, and the extent to which those actions
       and inactions were consistent with the prior Healthcare Action Plan; and
   (4) Recommendations for concrete actions that the City and its federal and state
       representatives can take to improve healthcare affordability and accessibility,
       insurance and benefit coverage, and medical service availability.

D. COMPOSITION OF THE CITIZENS’ ACTION COMMITTEE, QUORUM,
VOTING RULES

The Citizens’ Action Committee shall consist of seven (7) members to be appointed
by resolution of the City Commission for terms to be determined by the City
Commission. Members shall serve without compensation. The City Commission
shall select individuals who have the research, analytical, and community
engagement skills necessary to execute the powers and duties of the Citizens’ Action
Committee and produce a Healthcare Action Plan, and shall select members with
a broad array of backgrounds such as healthcare workers, uninsured or
underinsured community members, public health professionals, patient advocates,
and organized labor. Vacancies on the Citizens’ Action Committee may be filled by
the City Commission at any time. A majority of the appointed Citizens’ Action
Committee members constitutes a quorum, and action may be taken by a majority
vote of the members present. Members may be removed by the City Commission
for cause, or automatically after a specified number of unexcused absences.

E. POWERS AND DUTIES OF THE CITIZENS’ ACTION COMMITTEE

The Citizens’ Action Committee shall have the duty to:

(1) Hold multiple community forums each year for the purpose of hearing
    firsthand accounts of community members’ ability to afford and access
    primary, emergency, and necessary specialty medical care, in compliance with
    open meeting and public records requirements;
(2) Hold multiple community forums each year for the purpose of hearing views
    on how federal, state, and local policy changes have impacted healthcare
    affordability, accessibility, and service availability, in compliance with open
    meeting and public records requirements;
(3) Compile data relevant to assessing the current state of healthcare affordability,
    accessibility, and service availability;
(4) Assess how federal, state, and local policies and recent policy changes have
    impacted healthcare affordability, accessibility, and service availability;
(5) Identify how the City’s federal and state representatives voted on relevant
    policy decisions;
(6) Measure the City’s federal and state representatives’ actions or inactions
    against the prior Healthcare Action Plan; and
(7) Issue a Healthcare Action Plan that compiles its findings and makes
    recommendations.

The Citizens’ Action Committee shall have the power to partner with researchers,
research institutions, state and local agencies and departments, foundations, and
consultants to assist in its assessments and the performance of its duties.

The Citizens’ Action Committee shall elect from among its members a Chair and
Vice-Chair, and may also elect such other officers as it deems necessary. Officers

   shall serve for three years, or until their successors are elected and qualified.
   Officers shall be elected by majority vote of the committee at the first regular
   meeting of the year. Any vacancy in an officer position shall be filled by majority
   vote at the next regular meeting or special meeting called for that purpose. The
   Chair shall preside at meetings; the Vice-Chair shall perform the Chair’s duties
   when the Chair is absent or unable to act.

   F. PUBLICATION AND ACTION PLAN MEETING

   No later than October 1 of every even-numbered year, the City Commission shall
   publish the Citizens’ Action Committee’s Healthcare Action Plan,
   recommendations, and all relevant research and findings in a manner that achieves
   broad public engagement. Minority members may append dissenting views to the
   published plan. No later than October 15 of every even-numbered year, the City
   Commission shall hold a special meeting with the Citizens’ Action Committee to
   provide a public overview of the Healthcare Action Plan and to adopt a Healthcare
   Action Plan.

   G. ADMINISTRATION AND IMPLEMENTATION

   The City Commission shall facilitate the implementation of this Section. The
   Citizens’ Action Committee may adopt rules of procedure for the conduct of its
   meetings, the receipt of public comment, the preparation of reports, and the
   completion of the Healthcare Action Plan, consistent with the Charter and
   applicable laws.

   H. ETHICS, CONFLICT OF INTEREST

   The City Commission shall adopt a Conflict of Interest and/or Ethics Policy to
   govern the conduct of the members of the Citizens’ Action Committee.

   I. SEVERABILITY

   If any section, subsection, sentence, clause, or phrase of this Charter amendment is
   for any reason held invalid or unconstitutional, such decision shall not affect the
   validity of the remaining portions of this Charter amendment.
