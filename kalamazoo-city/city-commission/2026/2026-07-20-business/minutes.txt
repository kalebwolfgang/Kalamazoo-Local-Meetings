[Policy Items]

[Call-In Public Comments]
Vice Mayor Duncan, seconded by Commissioner Slaby, moved that the City Commission direct the City Attorney, in concert with the City Manager, to prepare alternative amendments to the rules and procedures concerning public comment. The alternatives should include options regarding the duration of individual public comments, opportunities for remote participation through call-in technology, and the placement of general comments on the meeting agenda, together with any related procedural changes necessary to implement these options. The City Clerk, City Manager, and City Attorney should also identify legal and operational considerations on public engagement associated with each alternative and return them to the City Commission for a vote.

Discussion followed. Commissioners requested the staff response and recommendation take into consideration and address the following:
    the current practices of the City's other boards, commissions, and committees regarding call-in public comments, and the need for consistency between the City Commission and these boards
    a proactive plan to deal with hate speech during public comments while upholding First Amendment rights
    the sections of the agenda where call-in comments would be allowed
    staffing considerations
    the length of individual public comments
    the position of general public comments on the agenda

City Manager Hankins indicated he had enough information and clarity to proceed with the City Commission's request, and he suggested Commissioners send him any additional questions and ideas they might have on the issue.

[New Business]

[Discussion re: a Moratorium on Data Centers]
Discussion took place regarding a moratorium on data centers.

Vice Mayor Duncan suggested the City explore the legality of a two-year moratorium on data centers. There were no objections.

City Attorney Leal indicated he could give a 15-20-minute presentation at a Committee of the Whole meeting that would focus on the definition of, and legal parameters for, moratoria.

Vice Mayor Duncan requested that City Attorney Leal give this presentation at the next Committee of the Whole meeting.

Commissioners suggested the following topics for exploration:
    the full range of tools available to help the community deal with data centers
    potential moratoria on other types of facilities, such as those involving cryptocurrency, artificial intelligence, and battery storage
    the intersection of this issue with the Imagine Kalamazoo 2035 Master Plan process
    the zoning tools currently available and the ones potentially available if the current zoning rules were changed

City Attorney Leal requested clarification on the scope of his presentation.

City Manager Hankins offered that a moratorium would give space for staff to study the topics Commissioners had mentioned.
