A Message from the City Manager
Summer is one of the busiest seasons for the City of Kalamazoo, and this year is no exception. Across our
community, we're making significant investments in infrastructure, expanding opportunities for resident
engagement, improving how we deliver City services, and planning for Kalamazoo's future through our FY2027
budget and Imagine Kalamazoo 2035.

Many of these projects require patience, but each represents a long-term investment in creating a stronger,
safer, and more connected community. Thank you to our residents, businesses, community partners, and City
employees for your continued support and engagement as we move this important work forward together.

Crosstown Parkway Center Nears Completion
Later this month, the City will officially open the Arthur Washington Jr. Building at Crosstown Parkway Center.
This new facility represents an important investment in customer service and organizational effectiveness. By
bringing Community Planning and Economic Development, Information Technology, Diversity, Equity and
Inclusion, and Communications together under one roof, we'll improve collaboration among staff while making
City services more accessible for residents and businesses. The project will also modernize parts of Kalamazoo
Public Safety Station 1.

Community Planning and Economic Development will temporarily close its current offices on July 27 before
reopening in the new facility on July 30. Renovations to the Public Safety portion of the campus continue and
are expected to be completed this fall.

Planning for New Housing at 322 Stockbridge
Housing continues to be one of our highest community priorities.
The City is moving forward with planning for approximately 17 acres at 322 Stockbridge, creating an
opportunity to transform a long-vacant property into a vibrant new neighborhood. Preliminary concepts
include a mix of single-family homes, cottage courts, townhomes, accessory dwelling units, and multifamily
housing while strengthening connections to the surrounding Edison neighborhood, parks, and trail system.
Community input will play an important role in shaping this development, and residents are encouraged to
participate in the online survey available in both English and Spanish at the following links: 322 Stockbridge
Community Survey or in Spanish at Encuesta de Renovación de 322 Stockbridge.

PLANNING FOR TOMORROW

FY2027 Budget Development Begins
Work is underway on the City's FY2027 budget. Departments across the organization are evaluating service
levels, operational priorities, and future investments that will shape the proposed budget presented to the City
Commission later this year. Equally important, we're expanding opportunities for residents to participate in the
process. A new public budget survey is launching this month, and staff are planning a community budget
meeting that will provide residents with an opportunity to better understand City finances, learn about the
budget process, and help inform future priorities. As with previous years, budget decisions will continue to be
guided by the community priorities identified through Imagine Kalamazoo while incorporating an equity-
focused approach to evaluating investments and service delivery.

Imagine Kalamazoo 2035
This summer marks another important step in developing Imagine Kalamazoo 2035. Staff plan to present an
update during a Committee of the Whole meeting in August, including results from the Design It! phase,
refinements to the community vision, and proposed strategic goals that will guide City priorities over the next
decade. The goal remains clear: establish a shared vision with measurable outcomes that help the City align
future investments, improve accountability, and strengthen services across every neighborhood.
Staff anticipate presenting the completed Strategic Vision to the City Commission for consideration this
September.

Kalamazoo Residents Academy
An exciting initiative currently under development is the Kalamazoo Residents Academy. The proposed
program is designed to help residents better understand how local government works while creating new
opportunities for civic leadership and community engagement. Participants would interact directly with
department leaders, tour City facilities, and gain a better understanding of the services that support our
community every day. Programs like this have proven successful at strengthening relationships between
residents and local government by offering pathways to meaningfully engage with City of Kalamazoo staff and
learn about City services and facilities. To accommodate the startup of the program, a budget amendment
request will be brought to City Commission to utilize funding toward starting to plan and design of the program
before 2027 ahead of a potential public launch in mid-2027.

IMPROVING CITY SERVICES

‘Kalamazoo & You’: Downtown Parking
The City of Kalamazoo recently announced new parking options in downtown Kalamazoo. In our public affairs
video series, Kalamazoo & You, Communications Manager Mike Smith talks with parking consultant Rob
Bacigalupi about the updated parking system. This update includes new digital kiosks that have replaced the
city’s old mechanical meters. This allows City staff to adjust rates and time limits more quickly and to collect
data on parking demand. The main goal is to make downtown parking more convenient and easier for
everyone. The video can be viewed on the City’s Facebook page here. Be sure to check out Public Media
Network and the City’s Facebook page for previous and upcoming Kalamazoo & You videos.

Understanding Boil Water Advisories
Water quality remains one of the City's highest priorities. Boil Water Advisories are issued as a precaution
whenever water system repairs could potentially affect water quality. In nearly every case, the advisories are
lifted within a few days after two separate rounds of laboratory testing confirm the water is safe.
Our Department of Public Services hand-delivers notifications whenever possible and works quickly to complete
testing so advisories can be removed as soon as conditions allow.

If the affected area is large and includes a lot of properties, advisories may be issued by media release. Here is

PARKS, RECREATION & QUALITY OF LIFE
Summer is in full swing, and Kalamazoo Parks continues to offer opportunities for residents of all ages.
Youth summer camps are operating throughout the community, while Police Athletic League programming
continues providing recreation and skill-building opportunities for young people. At Verburg Park, residents are
already enjoying the newly completed playground, pavilion, and accessible kayak launch. Construction of the
new pump track is expected to begin later this summer. Kik Pool is also experiencing another successful season,
with thousands of visitors enjoying the facility throughout June. Strong participation across our parks and
recreation programs reflects the important role these amenities play in supporting healthy, connected
neighborhoods.

CONNECTING WITH OUR COMMUNITY
One of the most rewarding parts of serving as City Manager is the opportunity to meet with residents,
neighborhood leaders, businesses, nonprofit organizations, and City employees throughout our community.
This month I had the privilege of celebrating the Kalamazoo Community Foundation's 100th anniversary and
recognizing the tremendous impact the organization has had on generations of residents.

I also joined community leaders in recognizing the retirement of Jeff Patton following his distinguished career
leading Integrated Services of Kalamazoo, attended the 40th Annual Black Arts Festival celebrating the
richness of our community, and met with City employee members of the City's Diversity, Equity and Inclusion
Council to discuss their work supporting an inclusive workplace and strengthening organizational culture.
These conversations help shape our work and reinforce the importance of maintaining strong partnerships
throughout the community.

AIR QUALITY ALERTS ACROSS MICHIGAN
Last week, staff closely monitored the statewide air quality event caused by wildfire smoke from Canada and
Minnesota. Updates from EGLE, MDHHS, and other official sources were shared through the City's website,
social media, emergency notification systems, and internal communications. Emergency Management also
coordinated with Kalamazoo County HCS to obtain N95 masks for vulnerable populations. The KDPS
Community Policing Division distributed approximately 80 masks to unhoused individuals who remained
outdoors during the event, with the remaining masks being kept at the Community Policing Office substation for
continued distribution by officers as needed. Staff will continue to monitor air quality alerts and conditions and
share timely information and resources to help keep our community safe.

LOOKING AHEAD
Over the coming weeks, residents can expect continued progress on several important initiatives, including the
opening of Crosstown Parkway Center, public engagement opportunities related to the FY2027 budget and
322 Stockbridge redevelopment, and the August update on Imagine Kalamazoo 2035.

Each of these efforts reflects our ongoing commitment to delivering excellent public services while thoughtfully
planning for Kalamazoo's future. Thank you for your continued partnership as we work together to build a
community where every neighborhood has the opportunity to thrive.
