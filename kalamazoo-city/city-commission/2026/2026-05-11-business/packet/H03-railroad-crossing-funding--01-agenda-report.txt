RECOMMENDATION:

That the City Commission accept a $3,225,000 funding package, including a Railroad Crossing
Elimination grant and related funding agreements. It is also recommended that the City
Commission approve a budget amendment in the amount of $3,225,000 for this grant and
delegate signatory authority for the agreement and subsequent reports to the Grants Division
Manager.

BACKGROUND:

The City of Kalamazoo has long experienced challenges in its Downtown Transportation
Network. In addition to a maze of high-speed, multi-lane, one-way streets which were built upon
historic redlining practices and divide the community, a complicated and inefficient train
network further causes significant congestion, delays, and pollution. All of these combine to
create burdens on our most disadvantaged neighborhoods and to prevent necessary and needed
economic growth. The city has committed to transformation and is redesigning and
reconstructing the downtown streets network – converting the one-way streets into two-way,
focusing on the needs of our pedestrians and multi-modal users, and removing barriers to access
through a variety of projects. The City of Kalamazoo must now address the train network and
find a solution that meets the needs of all users, improves efficiency, reduces congestion, reduces
environmental impacts, and supports a thriving economy. This grant will allow the City of
Kalamazoo to address two significant challenges associated with the railroads which traverse the
City of Kalamazoo and our region:

      • North-South Train Travel: Each train which comes south to Kalamazoo from Grand
        Rapids (and all points north) crosses both Kalamazoo and Michigan Avenues (major east-
        west routes across the city) at least twice to access Kalamazoo’s train yard, located on the
        eastern side of the city. They enter the city from the north, cross these streets, switch

Perhaps more significantly, this rail yard is currently located in a flood plain, adjacent to the
Kalamazoo River in an area classified by CEJST as disadvantaged. This area has flooded at least
five times since 2018 alone, carrying pollutants into the surrounding CEJST disadvantaged
community each and every time. While samples have not yet been taken on the site, with the
presence of a rail yard in this location since the 1800’s, it can be assumed that there is significant
contamination present. Typical rail line contamination includes metals, pesticides (such as lead
arsenate), and constituents of oil or fuel (petroleum products). These chemicals have been
associated with normal railroad operations and are likely to be found anywhere along the line but
are compounded at the rail yard. Other sources of contaminants associated with historic railroad
operation may include coal ash from engines, creosote from ties, and polynuclear aromatic
hydrocarbons (“PAHs”) from the diesel exhaust. Every single time this area floods, these
chemicals are washed into the river and poison it.

Through this planning grant, the City of Kalamazoo and its diverse partners and supporters will
develop, evaluate, and select an option to address these issues, and close several urban, high-
traffic railroad crossings in this process (see map). Two concepts have been proposed by the
stakeholders over time and require both evaluation and refinement:

     • Concept A involves closure of up to 10 railroad crossings and consolidation of
       duplicative track to the north of the city.
     • Concept B involves closure of up to 42 railroad crossings and consolidation of
       duplicative track to the south of the city.

Both concepts will eliminate one set of railroad crossings over Kalamazoo and Michigan Avenue
and relocate the rail yard to reduce its environmental impacts and the congestion created as trains
navigate the outdated yard. This relocation will ensure the yard is not located in a flood plain
and CEJST disadvantaged census tract. Additional concepts may also become apparent during
this planning grant and consideration will also be given to those concepts. The City of
Kalamazoo is willing to lead this process, but clearly understands that it will only truly be
feasible and successful if time, attention, and resources are dedicated to engaging all
stakeholders and creating the best possible solution which meets the needs of all users. Once
complete, this project will accomplish five major tasks as detailed below, building upon data and
need generated through comprehensive systems planning and assessed by the City of Kalamazoo
and its partners. The planning project will result in an intentional and evaluated plan to improve
safety, environmental health, and freight connectivity across the region:

     • a comprehensive description of the needs of all system users (freight/railroad, vehicular,
       pedestrian, and other multi-modal) will be documented as well as opportunities to be
       accomplished through the various conceptual designs,
     • project goals and objectives will be articulated, including environmental considerations to
       be utilized to assess the performance of all design concepts,
     • conceptual engineering will be completed as well as any other designs which assist in
       defining the solutions/designs under consideration,
     • specific consideration will be given to ensure any resulting design will avoid, minimize,
       and mitigate potential negative environmental effects on natural and human
       environmental resources—including how the project can actually improve upon the
       current environmental impact, and
     • a comprehensive evaluation will be conducted to assess how design concepts meet the
       goal and objectives, considering capital and operating cost implications.

Stakeholder engagement will be of paramount importance and will be incorporated in all aspects
of this project. To that end, the grant allocates funds to support the staffing required to
coordinate, consolidate, and ensure appropriate feedback is incorporated, as well as to manage
the entire project to ensure its success.

This planning grant, coupled with the related funding agreements, represents a critical and
time-sensitive first step toward advancing a comprehensive strategy to relocate the railyard
to a more suitable location—an action essential to improving public safety, reducing
pollution, alleviating traffic congestion and delays, and fostering a healthier community in
coordination with the broader Downtown Kalamazoo Transportation Network redesign.
This comprehensive plan is a requisite precursor to seeking and obtaining funding to make
this vision a reality.

Awards (3) for the closure of three railroad crossings. The motion to enter into negotiations with
MDOT regarding these closures was approved by City Commission on September 16, 2024 and
the agreements are attached. Both the grant and the match funding will be tracked in project
accounts which will be set up upon acceptance of the grant award. The acceptance of the grant
award is also the approval amending the budget.
