RECOMMENDATION:

It is recommended that the City Commission approve a contract supplemental/change order with

Upgrades contract in the amount of $414,447.28. It is recommended that the City Commission
approve a budget amendment in the amount of $414,447.28.

BACKGROUND:

Ongoing aesthetic water quality (taste, odor, color) issues have been experienced by residents at
Abbey 42 in cold and hot water fixtures since construction. Aesthetic water quality contributing
factors include water main oversizing, water age, low water usage, iron concentrations above
EPA secondary treatment standards, and operational factors during periods of increased
hydraulic demands within the High-Pressure District.

Iron reducing bacteria and sulfur reducing bacteria populations have propagated within the water
main due to system contributing factors. Iron reducing bacteria and sulfur reducing bacteria, if
not controlled, may contribute to instances of hydrogen sulfide gas odor above ATSDR MRL
and iron concentrations above health standards (above 2.0 mg/L).

Non-pathogenic bacteria and inorganic demands have and may continue to reduce system
chlorine residual at the point of entry below recommended concentrations of 0.2 mg/L.
Reduced chlorine residuals could contribute to increased risks for the propagation of pathogenic
bacteria and organisms.

Due to the population density of Abbey 42, water chemistry and sampling results, and
contributing factors of the system hydraulics and water distribution system at and near Abbey 42

Water Treatment Recommendations and Pilot Evaluations
Detailed lab analysis and independent evaluations by consultants resulted in recommendations
for mechanical filtration of cold water and cold water influent to water heaters, externally
powered titanium anode rods in water heaters, ASSE 1070 mixing valves at water heaters and
temperature adjustment of water heater storage temperature to 140 degrees F with outlet
distribution temperature below 120 degrees F.

The recommendations were approved by the City Commission on 9-15-2025 and pilot tested in a
group of 30 units. Pilot testing resulted in successful installation, use and functional
performance of the externally powered titanium anode rods in water heaters, ASSE 1070 mixing
valves at water heaters and temperature adjustment of water heater storage temperature to 140
degrees F with outlet distribution temperature below 120 degrees F improvements.

Given the success of the pilot, this recommendation to complete all outstanding installations of
externally powered titanium anode rods in water heaters and ASSE 1070 mixing valves in 264
units for a cost of $414,447.28. (total contract to date $629,163.48) is proposed.

The mechanical filtration portion of the pilot was not successful. Once installed, routine and
periodic maintenance of the mechanical filtration units on an individual unit basis proved to be
challenging due to several factors. Individual residents lacked the training and understanding of
mechanical filtration systems needed to identify common use fault scenarios such as clogged
filters, routine filter changes, pressure observations and bypass. Maintenance response and
scheduling for planned, corrective and demand maintenance proved problematic as individual
residents could not maintain the operational tempos required.

Mechanical Filtration Alternatives
Following unsuccessful piloting of unit mechanical filtration at Abbey 42, point of entry at the
building level and at the site level were evaluated. Point of entry treatment at the building level
would require 26 units at 13 locations, each providing redundant mechanical filtration of a 2”
diameter building domestic water service at the discharge side of the meter.

Treatment at the site level would require 2 units at one location providing redundant mechanical
filtration and chlorination of the 8” diameter site loop feeding all buildings. Station bypass and
pressure sustaining flow control valves would be used to maintain fire flow.

The mechanical filtration alternatives continue to be evaluated and may be brought forward for
future recommendations.
