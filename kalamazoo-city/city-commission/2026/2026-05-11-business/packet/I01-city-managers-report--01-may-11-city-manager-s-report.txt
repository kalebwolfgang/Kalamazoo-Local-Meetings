City Response to Strong Winds
In the spring, weather can wreak havoc, as we saw a few weeks ago with significant flooding in parts of
Kalamazoo. In late April, we dealt with thunderstorms and damaging high winds, which brought down power
lines and trees throughout the city. Meteorologists describe the strong wind gusts as a “wake low” – a
phenomena that occurs when air drives out behind rain showers and warms up, creating a small but intense
area of low pressure.

Crews from our Public Services, along with Public Safety officers, Public Safety Emergency Management, and
Consumers Energy sprang into action. They worked tirelessly in a coordinated effort to clear debris, restore
access to roadways, and assist residents as quickly and safely as possible. Public messaging on the recovery
was also distributed by both Public Safety and Emergency Management, and shared with City
Communications and County emergency management partners. All these efforts quickly moved the city
toward full restoration of normal operations. We thank all involved for their tireless work and quick response.

Farmers Market Season Opening
The Kalamazoo Farmers Market opened its season on Saturday, May 2. All indications are the opening day
launch was a major success. Farmers Market operators Gamsho Group, LLC reported the first day went very
well and the market opened smoothly, with all key operating systems functioning, including SNAP/EBT and
Double Up Food Bucks processing. Vendor check-in, booth placement, customer flow, and on-site operations
all went well for the first Saturday of the season. Operators also reported strong activation throughout the
market, including food trucks offering coffee and food which helped create a welcoming atmosphere for
customers and vendors. My family and I were able to visit the market for the first time and enjoyed the
connecting with people, festivities and some of our favorite local vendors.

City of Kalamazoo Holds Public Services Week
Ever wondered what it takes to keep Kalamazoo running or how wastewater is cleaned? The answer can be
found by attending this year’s Public Services Week open house activities Tuesday, May 19, and Thursday, May
21. The City of Kalamazoo’s Department of Public Services invites the public to go behind the scenes with
Public Services staff at the department’s facilities. The Kalamazoo Water Reclamation Plant is located at 1415
Harrison Street, and the Public Works division is located at 415 Stockbridge Avenue. KWRP houses Wastewater,
Environmental Services, Process Control, and Sewer Collections as well as Water Supply. The Stockbridge
facility houses Water Distribution, Records, Public Works which include Asphalt, Stormwater/Concrete, Forestry,
and Citywide Maintenance, as well as the Support Services Division which includes Fleet.

Public Services Week is the public’s chance to see and experience the people, places, and equipment that
power our community. There will be activities for all ages, including truck displays and presentations on Public
Services facilities. Snacks and refreshments, as well as raffles and giveaways, will be available at the facilities as
well. More information on Public Services Week can be found at
https://www.kalamazoocity.org/Community/Events-Meetings/Public-Services-Week.

Second Wave Media Series on ‘Streets for All’
Kalamazoo has a fascinating history. Our streets have always played an important role in our history. As we
begin this summer the transformation of downtown streets from one-way to two-way, the City of Kalamazoo
has partnered with Second Wave Media to sponsor a series of articles on the Streets for All initiative, the
ongoing transformation of downtown Kalamazoo, and our ever-changing city. The first article in the series is
titled “From ancestral trails to two-way roads: How Kalamazoo streets keep changing with the times” and is
written by local freelance journalist Mark Wedel. The article is on the Second Wave Media web page:
https://secondwavemedia.com/from-ancestral-trails-to-two-way-roads-how-kalamazoo-streets-keep-
changing-with-the-times/ Look for ongoing series articles on Kalamazoo streets from Second Wave Media.

The Streets for All two-way downtown initiative will soon get underway. The Kalamazoo Avenue project is now
out to bid, and once that is complete, construction on Kalamazoo Avenue is expected to begin shortly after
the Fourth of July. Work will start on Kalamazoo Avenue at Park Street and move east. This phase includes a full
reconstruction of the street, upgrading sidewalks, lighting, utilities, and adding street trees. To help minimize
disruptions to businesses, residents, and traffic, construction will be completed in shorter segments as crews
move along the corridor.

Bike Week in the City of Kalamazoo
May is National Bike Month, and the City of Kalamazoo is rolling out a variety of events to celebrate cycling!
The 15th annual Kalamazoo Bike Week starts Saturday, May 9, with Bikes in the Zoo. This year’s event takes
place from 11 a.m. to 2 p.m. on the downtown Kalamazoo Mall. The event is open to all ages, and no
registration is needed. The annual Bike Rodeo takes place from 5 to 6:30 p.m. Wednesday, May 13. This year’s
event is at a new location – the Kalamazoo Farmers Market, located at 1204 Banks St. Information on the Bike
Rodeo, and a chance to enter an online drawing to win a new bike is available at
www.kzooparks.org/BikeRodeo.

Bike to Work Day is on Friday, May 15, a day to trade four wheels for two and experience the benefits of biking
to work. Saturday, May 16 is the annual Mayors’ Bike Ride from 10 to 11 a.m., led by the mayors of the City of
Portage and the City of Kalamazoo. The free event starts and ends at the Kalamazoo Farmers Market and is a
short, recreational neighborhood ride to promote community bicycling and the partnership between the cities
of Kalamazoo and Portage. The Mayors’ Ride will be held rain or shine unless there is lightening or other
inclement weather.

Kalamazoo Area Bike Week is presented by Bike Friendly Kalamazoo (BFK), a Michigan non-profit organization
dedicated to enhancing regional quality of life through bicycling. More information on Kalamazoo Area Bike
Week can be found at www.bikefriendlykalamazoo.org.

Memorial Day Observance

Boil Water Advisories
Boil Water Advisories are precautionary. They are issued while we test to confirm the water has not been
affected. Our team completes two separate rounds of testing before advisories are lifted. While under a Boil
Water Advisory, affected customers are advised to boil their tap water for two minutes before consuming it, or
use bottled water until the advisory is lifted. No special actions are needed for water used for hygiene. The
vast majority of Boil Water Advisories are issued out of an abundance of caution and lifted within three days.

The Kalamazoo Department of Public Services hand delivers water advisory notices to affected homes and
businesses whenever possible. If the affected area is large and includes a lot of properties, advisories may be
issued by media release. Here is a list of watermain breaks and boil water advisories for the weeks of April 20-

Buzz in the Zoo
Kzoo Parks and the Audubon Society of Kalamazoo are joining forces for a family-friendly World Migratory Bird
Day Celebration. This free event will be held from 9 a.m. until 2 p.m. Saturday, May 16, at Upjohn Park. It
features activities for all ages, including a guided bird walk, live raptor program, hands-on crafts, a nature art
market, and more. This year’s theme, “Every Bird Counts – Your Observations Matter,” highlights how everyone
can play a role in protecting birds.

The event also marks the kickoff of Buzz in the Zoo, a City of Kalamazoo initiative that encourages residents to
create wildlife-friendly yards. By planting native species, removing invasive plants, and using natural materials,
even small changes at home can support pollinators and local ecosystems. Throughout the season, residents
can take part in hands-on events and pick up yard signs to show their support. Learn more at
www.kalamazoocity.org/BuzzInTheZoo.

The Landing Place
I appreciated the opportunity to join many of our community partners on April 30 to celebrate the opening of

Connecting With Our Community
Over the past several weeks, I have had the opportunity to continue participating in community meet-and-
greet events coordinated by ISAAC (Interfaith Strategy for Advocacy & Action in the Community). These
conversations and gatherings have provided valuable opportunities to connect directly with residents and
community leaders across Kalamazoo. Last month, I spent time with residents in the Northside neighborhood
through an event hosted by the Douglas Community Association. This month, I had the opportunity to meet
with community members at Urban Alliance and attend an Urban Alliance Change of Status Graduation
Ceremony. It was an inspiring experience to witness individuals intentionally committing themselves to personal
growth, transformation, and continued development. I also had the opportunity to meet with Kalamazoo
residents and members of the African immigrant community to hear directly from them about opportunities to
strengthen partnerships, and address challenges and conditions uniquely impacting immigrant families and
residents in our community.

I recognize the effort, planning, and collaboration required to organize these engagements and greatly
appreciate the thoughtful and intentional work of ISAAC, Urban Alliance, and the many community
organizations, individuals and leaders helping create spaces for meaningful dialogue and connection.

Community Cleanup Opportunities
Residents interested in organizing neighborhood cleanups can partner with the City of Kalamazoo for support
and resources. The City is assisting community-led cleanup efforts focused on litter removal, recycling, and the
removal of invasive species. To support these efforts, the City can provide equipment and supplies including
safety vests, gloves, buckets, trash bags, trash grabbers, and recycling and trash dumpsters.
Residents interested in coordinating a cleanup effort can contact Chris Broadbent, Solid Waste Coordinator, at
broadbentc@kalamazoocity.org.
