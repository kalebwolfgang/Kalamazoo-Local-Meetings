Continuous Decline in Traffic Crashes
We continue to make progress in our efforts to make our streets safe. Last year marked another year of
sustained improvement in city-wide traffic safety, according to finalized crash data compiled from Michigan
State Police records. Total reported crashes in 2025 were 2,339, a decrease from 2,530 in 2024 and a 24.4
percent reduction compared to the 2014–2019 pre-pandemic average of 3,093 crashes per year.

Over the past three years (2023–2025), Kalamazoo’s Safe Streets for All initiatives are estimated to have
prevented approximately 1,715 crashes, compared to expected totals based on historical averages. And from
2024 to 2025 alone, total crashes declined by 191 crashes, or 7.5 percent, continuing our steady downward
trajectory.

The data show that this is not a one-year fluctuation. We are seeing a sustained downward trend in crashes
and injuries. The consistency of the improvement tells us that Safe Streets for All Safety Action Plan investments
are working. We adopted the Safe Streets for All Safety Action Plan to guide future transportation safety
improvements in Kalamazoo. The action plan is Kalamazoo’s commitment to enhance a safe network for
walking, biking, riding, and driving throughout the city. It is a continuation of work that began in 2024 and
continued through last year, and unlocks additional federal funding for infrastructure improvements.

This year, the City of Kalamazoo will continue to apply safety and calming measures across the city. Our
emphasis on the number of people we can prevent from death or getting seriously injured and needing
medical attention is the real important point. We remain steadfast in touting safety as our primary objective.

Downtown Parking Update
There is good news for your morning routine in Downtown Kalamazoo: paid parking on the Kalamazoo Mall
now begins at 10 a.m. That means you have two extra morning hours to stop by, run errands, or grab coffee
before paid parking begins. This change is part of a series of parking updates happening this spring to better
align with how and when parking is used and to help meet demand downtown. Look for signs promoting the
new times that are posted around Downtown and along the Downtown Mall.

And remember: There’s always 90 minutes FREE in the Kalamazoo Mall and Epic Center parking ramps, located
just steps off the Downtown Mall. You can learn more about spring parking updates at www.parkkalamazoo.
org.

Downtown Community Update
In the early morning hours of Thursday, February 19, several downtown businesses, along with the First Presbyterian
Church on South Street and the Ladies’ Library Association building on South Park Street, experienced vandalism when
an individual moved through the area breaking windows.

The Kalamazoo Department of Public Safety (KDPS) responded quickly. Utilizing recently implemented BlueLight
cameras and FUSUS technology, officers were able to identify and locate the suspect, who was apprehended
approximately 36 minutes after the first call. Following the incident, the Downtown Ambassador team visited affected
businesses and organizations to offer assistance with cleanup, debris removal, and to provide on-site support where
needed. Their outreach was focused on ensuring business owners had access to help and understood available
resources.

I contacted most of the affected businesses personally last week, speaking with them about the damage, their
immediate needs, and how the City can continue to support them. These conversations were important in
understanding the impact and identifying the next practical steps. I also hope to connect directly with representatives
from First Presbyterian Church also impacted by the incident.

The City of Kalamazoo remains committed to supporting downtown businesses and other affected properties as they
recover. Staff are reviewing response protocols and strategies to address incidents of this nature and to strengthen
coordination moving forward. Downtown businesses play a vital role in our community, and we appreciate their
resilience and partnership as we move forward together.

Women’s History Month
The City of Kalamazoo is proud to recognize March as Women’s History Month, celebrating women’s
contributions with the theme “Leading the Change: Women Shaping a Sustainable Future.” The Women’s
History Month observance dates to March 8, 1857, when hundreds of women from New York City garment
and textile factories rallied to protest harsh working conditions. As we celebrate Women’s History Month, we
reflect upon the advances women have made, including increased earnings, educational attainment, and job
opportunities. Learn more at Women’s History Month.

Staff Engagement
I continue to prioritize regular engagement with City staff to strengthen collaboration and deepen my
understanding of operations across the organization.

In January, I visited the Public Safety headquarters for a tour and operational briefing with the Kalamazoo
Department of Public Safety. I also participated in a day-and-a-half Senior Leadership Team retreat, where
I was introduced to City department directors and met with staff to discuss priorities connected to Imagine
Kalamazoo 2035.

Last month, I joined staff for a focused deep-dive session on the Foundation for Excellence and held individual
meetings with City appointees to better understand their roles, goals, and current initiatives.

Work continues on the renovation project at 150 East Crosstown Parkway. Last week, I toured the facility with
Public Safety to see the progress being made. The renovated space will house City of Kalamazoo departments:
Community Planning and Economic Development; Information Technology; Diversity, Equity and Inclusion; and
Communications – along with expanded space for Public Safety. As the project completion nears sometime
this spring, I have asked Public Safety leadership to schedule a time with the City Commission to tour the facility,
likely sometime this month.

Throughout March, I plan to continue these efforts through department briefings with directors, deputy
directors, and key personnel. These meetings will include facility visits and community tours to further build
operational insight and maintain strong working relationships across departments.

Connecting With Our Community
I continue to connect regularly with City staff through briefings, updates, and facility visits, while also making
it a priority to get out into the community, meet people where they are, and stay accessible to residents,
businesses, and local organizations. Over the past several weeks, I have visited and met with a wide range of
local businesses, nonprofit organizations, neighborhood leaders, and residents, often sitting down one-on-one,
or to listen and learn. I have also spent time in many of our downtown and neighborhood restaurants and small
businesses, recognizing the important role they play in the vitality of the City of Kalamazoo.

Most recently, I attended “The State of Kalamazoo: A Conversation Among Black Men,” hosted by the
Kalamazoo Black Male Alliance. The gathering brought together community members, nonprofit leaders,
educators, program partners, faith leaders, and advocates to examine the current realities facing Black boys
and men in Kalamazoo and to discuss how we can better align our efforts moving forward. The conversation
created space for honest dialogue, shared learning, and thoughtful action.

I also had the opportunity to attend a community meet and greet hosted by El Concilio with Commissioner
Slaby. The event provided a valuable opportunity to engage with many community members representing
a wide range of interests, perspectives, and areas of focus. I appreciate the hospitality of CEO Adrian
Vazquez and his leadership team bringing together a diverse group for meaningful conversation about the
organization’s ongoing work and the broader needs of our community.

Along with the Vice Mayor, I had the chance to tour the Kalamazoo Regional Educational Service Agency
Career Connect Campus. Walking through the facility and seeing students actively engaged in hands-on
programs was inspiring. I was struck by how the campus brings together so many career pathways in one
space, giving students real-world experience while helping them explore potential future careers. The visit
offered a valuable perspective on how local schools, businesses, and community partners are working together
to connect learning directly to opportunities after graduation.

Foundation For Excellence Dashboard Updated
The Foundation for Excellence (FFE) has updated its public investment dashboard to include 2025 data. The
dashboard now reflects more than $200 million in tracked investments in Kalamazoo. More than $111 million
has reduced property taxes for City taxpayers. Nearly $90 million has been invested directly into community
projects, programs, and essential City services.

The dashboard provides transparency regarding allocations across neighborhood improvements, programs,
and other initiatives. The updated dashboard is available at www.kalamazooffe.org/dashboard.

Water Main and Treatment Facility Project Resumes in Eastside
Construction continues on the City of Kalamazoo’s $60 million Water Treatment Plant and Water Main project.
Water main installation has resumed, beginning with closures on Elder and Trimble (south of E. Main). Water
main work along E. Michigan Avenue and Wallace Avenue was completed in 2025. Remaining water main
installation (continuing north to connect at Spring Valley Park) is scheduled to be completed this year.

Construction of the new 15,000-square-foot treatment facility at Station 5 began last October. Mass grading
is complete, and crews are installing storm sewer and underground electrical systems. Foundation work is
expected to begin in the coming months. The treatment plant remains on track for completion in 2028. Upon
completion, the facility will remove iron, manganese, and PFAS from the City’s water supply, consolidate
treatment operations, and strengthen reliability ahead of new federal PFAS regulations taking effect in 2029.

You can read more about this project at www.kalamazoocity.org/station5-14.
