BACKGROUND:

Self-Contained Breathing Apparatus (SCBA) units are a critical component of firefighter
personal protective equipment and are required for personnel operating in Immediately
Dangerous to Life or Health (IDLH) environments such as structural fires, hazardous materials
incidents, confined spaces, and other atmospheres that are toxic or oxygen deficient. The SCBA
units provide firefighters with a breathable air supply while protecting them from smoke, toxic
gases, carcinogens, and oxygen-deficient environments encountered during emergency response.

The SCBA units currently in service with the Kalamazoo Department of Public Safety were
purchased in 2012 and are now more than two National Fire Protection Association (NFPA)
standards cycles old. Fire service respiratory protection standards evolve regularly to incorporate
improvements in firefighter safety, materials, electronics, and accountability systems. Equipment
that falls multiple standards cycles behind current requirements no longer reflects modern safety
expectations and becomes increasingly difficult to maintain due to discontinued parts and
outdated technology.

Industry best practice and manufacturer guidance generally establish a 15-year operational
service life for SCBA systems. This lifecycle accounts for wear associated with emergency
operations, the deterioration of components exposed to heat and contaminants, evolving safety
standards, and the increasing cost and difficulty of maintaining aging equipment. The

The proposed replacement units are fully compliant with the NFPA 1970 Standard on Protective
Ensembles for Structural and Proximity Fire Fighting and Related Emergency Operations, which
consolidates multiple firefighter protective equipment standards into a single comprehensive
standard governing firefighter personal protective equipment systems.

Modern SCBA systems include significant safety and operational improvements, including
integrated PASS (Personal Alert Safety System) alarms, enhanced heads-up displays, voice
amplification systems, improved thermal resistance, and electronic air-management monitoring
capabilities that assist incident commanders in maintaining firefighter accountability and
situational awareness during emergency operations.

The Kalamazoo Department of Public Safety responds to approximately 14,000 fire, emergency
medical services, and rescue incidents annually, many of which require personnel to operate in
environments where respiratory protection is essential. Reliable SCBA equipment is therefore
critical to maintaining safe and effective emergency response operations.

The purchase through a Sourcewell cooperative purchasing contract allows the City to obtain
competitive pricing through a nationally solicited and publicly bid cooperative procurement
process. Use of the cooperative contract complies with the City’s purchasing policies while
ensuring best-value pricing and efficient procurement.
