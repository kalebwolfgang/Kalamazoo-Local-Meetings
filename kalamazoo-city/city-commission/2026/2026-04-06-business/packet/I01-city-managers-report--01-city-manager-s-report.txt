Imagine Kalamazoo 2035 “Design It!” Downtown Meetings in April
The March “Design It!” phase of Imagine Kalamazoo 2035 meetings is over, but the community dialogue
continues in April with multiple-day downtown Design It! events. These activities will kick off on the evening of
April 28, and continue through May 1 in conjunction with Art Hop. Residents can learn more about IK2035 by
visiting the website at www.kalamazoocity.org/IK2035.

Residents can also help shape Kalamazoo’s future by participating in Design It! online, on their time and from
anywhere, with activities that mirror the in-person Design It! meetings. Residents can go to
www.kalamazoocity.org/IKonline and take a quick activity, share a few ideas, or dive deeper and give
detailed feedback. Every response will help create the Imagine Kalamazoo 2035 plan.

Boil Water Advisories
Boil Water Advisories are precautionary. They are issued while we test to confirm the water has not been
affected. Our team completes two separate rounds of testing before advisories are lifted. While under a Boil
Water Advisory, affected customers are advised to boil their tap water for two minutes before consuming it, or
use bottled water until the advisory is lifted. No special actions are needed for water used for hygiene. The vast
majority of Boil Water Advisories are issued out of an abundance of caution and lifted within three days.

The Kalamazoo Department of Public Services hand delivers water advisory notices to affected homes and
businesses whenever possible. If the affected area is large and includes a lot of properties, advisories may be
issued by media release. Here is a list of water main breaks and boil water advisories for the weeks of March
16-March 30.

Kalamazoo shows improvements in community sustainability
Earth Day and Arbor Day observances are coming up, and we are pleased to report that our city is improving
in efforts to achieve community sustainability and address climate action. Staff from Public Services are
preparing Earth Day and Arbor Day proclamations to be read at the April 20 Kalamazoo City Commission 7
p.m. Regular Business Meeting. Our Community Sustainability Plan (CSP) is a forward-looking plan that guides
Kalamazoo’s

• Strategic Vision for a green and healthy city. Some of the improvements in sustainability include:
• Residential recycling collections, which totaled 2,698 tons in 2025, as well as launching a pilot food scrap
  recycling program.
• Establishing compost tumblers in community gardens at various neighborhood locations.
• Collecting materials at EPS foam and electronics drop off events.
• Switching the city’s vehicles to Biodiesel 11 fuel, which saved 277 metric tons of CO2e (carbon dioxide
  equivalent) – the equivalent to planting 4,580 trees within ten years.

Tree Canopy
A highlight of our community sustainability is promoting the city’s tree canopy. Trees remain a prominent focus
in Kalamazoo’s overall sustainability and environmental initiatives. Earlier today, Public Services crews planted
and dedicated a tree in Bronson Park in honor of the City Commission’s support for the environment. Each
year, our crews plant between 300 to 500 trees, investing half a million dollars per year in this effort, and trim
many other trees to prevent damage to protect residents from injury. We also provide a City of Kalamazoo Tree
Manual that guides the community in the best practices for planting, maintaining, and protecting trees in an
urban environment.

Yard waste pickup
Monthly yard waste collection begins this month and continues through October, with pickup once a month
on the scheduled day for your address. Residents can place prepared yard waste at the curb the night before
pickup.

New this year: What used to be April Spring Cleanup and May–October brush pickup has been combined
into one Yard Waste program. The same materials will now be accepted throughout the entire April–October
season.

Yard waste must be in biodegradable bags or boxes, or bundled with twine (loose material will not be
collected). Residents can place sticks, twigs, brush, and other yard waste at the curb on your scheduled
collection day. Find the schedule and full details at kalamazoocity.org/yardwaste.

Park Kalamazoo
Updated downtown parking changes began April 1, aimed at improving access, availability, and overall ease
of use. The updates include new multi-space kiosks, updated signage, and a revised system that better aligns
pricing with demand to reduce circling and improve turnover on the busiest blocks. As part of the changes,
12 high-demand downtown blocks will transition to paid parking, while 11 blocks will become free. The system
will include two pricing zones, with a $2/hour premium rate in the core and a reduced $1.50/hour base rate
on surrounding streets. There are 53 existing loading zones downtown that will also be converted to 15-minute
spaces, with additional short-term spaces added to support quick stops for deliveries, shopping, and takeout.
Paid parking will be available through kiosks and the ParkMobile app, with enforcement beginning at 10 a.m.
Ninety-minutes free parking will remain in City ramps.
