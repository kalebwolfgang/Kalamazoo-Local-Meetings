RECOMMENDATION:

It is recommended that the City Commission approve an agreement modification to the
previously approved $700,000 Treasury SLFRF/ARPA grant to a deferred loan for the
Kalrecovery Project, per the request of Kalrecovery I Limited Dividend Housing Association
Limited Partnership, to construct a new 48-unit housing project to include 46 permanent
supportive housing units for those recovering from opiate and methamphetamine use disorders.

BACKGROUND:

The City Commission approved these dollars for the Kalrecovery project on August 21, 2024 as
a grant to the developer. At the request of the Kalrecovery I Limited Dividend Housing
Association Limited Partnership (LDHALP), these dollars have been repackaged as a deferred
loan for City Commission's consideration. No additional Treasury SLFRF/ARPA dollars are
being recommended for approval. Delays in the construction of this development have been
solely due to the complex funding structure and the layering of subsidies at the federal, state and
local level that is needed to finance the project. As this project is at the end of securing its capital
(financial) stack for development, it has been communicated to the city that equity investors
supporting the project have raised concerns about accepting grants. The loan provided by the
City will be deferred for a 30-year affordability period required through a promissory note and
affordable housing deed restriction.

The Kalrecovery project continues Hollander Development’s record of developing creative
housing solutions to meet community needs. The project brings together a broad range of
partners to implement an innovative model for recovery housing focused on individuals and
families that have been impacted by substance-use disorders. Upon completion, the development

The development team for Kalrecovery has been awarded Low Income Housing Tax Credits
through the Michigan State Housing Development Authority (MSHDA). Therefore, all 46 of the
permanent supportive housing units will benefit low-to-moderate-income individuals or families
as soon as they are available for lease. Rental rates for one-, two-, and three-bedroom units are
intended to be accessible to households earning up to 30% and/or 50% of Area Median Income
(AMI). Additionally, the owners of the project will maintain rent and income restrictions on all
units, preserving affordable housing over the long-term.

Demographic analysis of participants in the Men’s and Women’s Drug Treatment Courts has
shown that a substantial number of participants earn less than 30% of the area median income. It
is also likely that many of the potential residents of the Kalrecovery project will meet the
Department of Housing and Urban Development’s definition of Category 1, 2, or 4
homelessness. To help address these dynamics, the development team will use MSHDA Project-
based Vouchers (PBVs) for the recovery housing units.

Increasing numbers of people with substance-use disorders are impacted by the criminal justice
system. Regional behavioral health systems and Michigan’s Problem-Solving Courts have
proven effective in promoting short-term recovery and reducing criminal recidivism. However, a
major barrier to long-term recovery for individuals participating in voluntary or court-ordered
programs is a lack of permanent supportive housing. The Kalrecovery permanent supportive
housing development helps to meet this need.

STRATEGIC VISION ALIGNMENT:

Shared Prosperity - Abundant opportunities for all people to prosper

This agreement will support the Shared Prosperity goal of the Imagine Kalamazoo Strategic Plan
as it results in permanent supportive and affordable housing for residents in crises. Achieving
this goal requires focus and collaboration among a spectrum of partners and placing a strong
focus on wraparound services. This is a primary component of the Kalrecovery projects,
assisting residents with long-term success addressing their substance-use disorder. Finally, the
Shared Prosperity goal encourages coordination of services and resources to support people
returning from prison. The project will utilize the Kalamazoo County courts as a priority to
connect residents with available units.

FISCAL IMPACT:

The commitment of these funds was originally approved on August 21, 2024. The conversion
from a grant to a deferred loan will mean the City of Kalamazoo will receive repayment of these
funds thirty years after the Affordable Housing Restrictive Covenant is recorded, following
project completion.
