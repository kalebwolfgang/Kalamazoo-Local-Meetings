RECOMMENDATION:

Staff recommends the approval of an ORDINANCE to rezone the property at 4301 Stadium
Drive from Residential, Multi-Dwelling (RM-15) and Residential, Single-Dwelling (RS-5) to
Community Commercial (CC).

BACKGROUND:

Property Background. 4301 Stadium Drive is an 18.8-acre parcel along the western border of the
City of Kalamazoo at the corner of Drake Road and Stadium Drive. The parcel is wooded and
slopes downwards towards the south-east with some ridges and slopes. The 18.8 acres is
currently dual-zoned with Residential Multi-Dwelling (RM-15) zoning along the Stadium Drive
frontage, and the majority of the area is zoned Residential Single-Dwelling (RS-5). The property
is also zoned with the Natural Features Protection Overlay (NFP-O). The applicant is requesting
that the property be rezoned from its current residential districts to the Community Commercial
(CC) District.

History of Zoning Requests. Rezoning this property has been requested previously. In January
2020, the property owner requested a rezoning of the property to the Community Commercial
(CC) District from its current residential zoning, which applied to the then three parcels, and
requested the removal of the NFP-O District. The Planning Commission reviewed the requests at
a public meeting and provided an unfavorable recommendation on the requests to the City
Commission for their consideration. However, at the applicant's request, the case did not advance
to the City Commission, and the legislative review body took no action on that request. In
September 2023, the applicant again applied to rezone the property from the RM-15 and RS-5
Districts to the CC District, but this time without the removal of the NFP-O District. This
application was withdrawn before the public hearing by the Planning Commission.

Fall 2025 Planning Commission Review. In the fall of 2025, following their application, the

the Planning Commission heard this case, City staff received a volume of written comments on
the request to rezone. The Planning Commission held a public hearing on the rezoning request at
City Hall on November 6th, 2025. At this meeting, City staff provided an overview of the
application and recommended that the Commission approve the request, outlining how it met the
zoning criteria used to consider rezoning applications.

There was a lengthy public comment period with speakers against the request and a full City
Commission Chambers of interested residents. After the public comment period, the Planning
Commission discussed the request, the application, and testimony provided during the public
hearing. The Planning Commission made their unanimous vote not to recommend support of the
request to rezone the property to the City Commission.

Review Process of Rezoning Requests (Zoning Map Changes). All rezoning requests first go to
the Planning Commission. They provide a recommendation (positive or negative) to the City
Commission. The City Commission enters the item into the record at a first reading and the
public hearing date is set for consideration. At the second meeting, the scheduled public hearing,
the City Commission will have presentations from staff and the applicant. Public comment will
be heard from the community before considering the request.

Review Criteria. When considering a rezoning request to change from one district to another, in
this case from Residential Single Family (RS-5) to Community Commercial (CC), is appropriate
in the location, the City Commission will review the following criteria per Chapter 8 of the
Zoning Ordinance:

1. The extent to which the amendment is consistent with the Master Plan 2025 and
Strategic Vision.

      The proposed rezoning request is consistent with the Strategic Vision and Master Plan,
      specifically the 2025 Master Plan Future Land Use Plan. The Future Land Use Plan details
      appropriate future land use designations and is a state-required element of the Master Plan.
      The Future Land Use Plan designates the property at the southeast corner of Drake Road
      and Stadium Drive as commercial with natural features protection. In creating this
      designation, the Master Plan considered surrounding land uses on the adjacent lots and
      block, and the adjacent street network. The alignment of land use and transportation was a
      key principle of the 2025 Master Plan. The 2025 Future Land Use Plan and the Master
      Plan principle of aligning transportation and land use have been used to update the City's
      Zoning Code over the last 6 years, including the changes that established a Natural
      Features Protection (NFP-0) overlay district and standards and form-based zoning code
      standards in Chapter 50 Zoning Code.

2. The extent to which there are changed conditions that require an amendment.

  Staff has determined that there are changed conditions that support the requested zoning map
  amendment. To make this determination, staff consider a number of factors detailed below.

  a. Can the property at present be reasonably used for any use allowed in the current zoning
  district? The current zoning designation primarily supports the development of single-family
  residential housing, specifically on its own lot and owner-occupied housing.

  Low-density residential is a challenging development use in this location. Low-density
  residential is not in alignment with the existing street types; both Drake Road and Stadium
  Drive are designated as Community Connectors in the Street Design Manual. This street type
  supports the highest traffic volumes and speeds. The Drake and Stadium intersection is a
  regional crossing that serves high-speed traffic, multiple transit lines, and land uses. The
  majority of the Stadium Drive frontage is auto-oriented commercial, reflective of the high-
  speed, high-volume suburban corridor. The property also serves two bus routes for K-Metro,
  which experience steady ridership numbers at the surrounding and adjacent bus stops.

  The automobile and transit volumes provide an ability to service higher-capacity uses than the
  existing residential zoning district allows. Low-density residential is out of character with the
  rest of the Stadium Drive corridor, both to the west towards the expressway and east toward
  Downtown. The 2025 Master Plan prioritizes alignment between streets and adjacent land
  uses.
