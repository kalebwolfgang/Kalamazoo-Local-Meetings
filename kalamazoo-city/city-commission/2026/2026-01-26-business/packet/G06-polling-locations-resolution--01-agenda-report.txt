SUBJECT:        Resolution Designating Polling Facility Locations

RECOMMENDATION:

It is recommended that the City Commission adopt a resolution designating polling facilities to
be used for the City's voting precincts during the election on May 5, 2026 and for each election
thereafter.

BACKGROUND:

Summary

On December 17th the City Election Commission (the City Clerk, City Attorney, and City
Assessor) approved changes to voting precinct boundaries to reduce the overall number of
precincts from 27 to 20. This was done at the request of the City Clerk in an effort to reduce
costs and staff workloads related to in-person, election day voting. The changes approved by the
Election Commission include the combination of 7 pairs of precincts and the renumbering of 3
precincts. At this time, it is recommended that the City Commission adopt a resolution that will:
1) establish polling locations for the combined and renumbered precincts; 2) change the polling
location for one precinct that was not combined or renumbered; and 3) confirm the polling
locations for precincts that have no changes. When the proposed polling facilities are considered
from the standpoint of the voter, only the voters in two precincts — Precinct 15 and the former
Precinct 23 — will be impacted by a change in location. All other voters will continue voting at
their current polling facilities. Administratively, this action needs to be taken to align the polling
locations with the new precincts and precinct numbers.

Precinct 14 Changes

The Election Commission combined precincts 20 and 23 and assigned the number "14" to the
new precinct. Prior to being combined, these precincts did not vote at the same location. It is
proposed that the new, combined precinct vote at Centerpoint City Church on Cork Street, which
is where the former Precinct 20 has been voting. The voters in former Precinct 23 (approximately
1,117 active voters) will be changing polling locations.

Precinct 15 Changes

It is recommended that Precinct 15 be moved from its current polling location at the Urban
Alliance facility on E. Stockbridge Avenue to the Bennet Building at St. Joseph Church on Lake
Street. The accessible entrance at Urban Alliance is not well positioned, both externally relative
to the parking lot, and internally relative to the room used for voting. Moreover, Urban Alliance
wants all outer doors to remain locked at all times. As a result, extra workers and signage are
needed just to get voters from the parking lot to the door, let them in the door, and lead them to
the polling place.

The City has been using the Bennet Building at St. Joseph Church as a polling location for many
years. It is a large space inside that can accommodate another precinct. It has adequate parking
and easy access to the building from the outside, and direct access into the polling place from the
outside entrance. This proposed location is only two blocks away from the current polling
location at Urban Alliance. Approximately 2,148 active voters would be affected by this change.

Communication to Voters

There will be no broad communication of these changes, as the information to be communicated
is voter-specific. All voters in the combined precincts, the renumbered precincts, and Precinct 15
will be sent new Voter Identification Cards that indicate their precinct # and/or polling location
may have changed. In addition, signs will be posted at the old polling locations for Precincts 15
and 23 on election days through 2027, directing voters to their new polling facilities.

COMMUNITY ENGAGEMENT:

Appropriate Depth of Engagement

Inform (one-way conversation) – no feasible alternatives available due to legal/regulatory
restrictions, standards and best practices, or resource limitations; the community will be made
aware of the project.

Discussion: Michigan Election Law requires polling facilities to be accessible under the
Americans with Disabilities Act, regardless of whether a facility is "grandfathered" and not
required to comply for the day-to-day use of the property. In addition, the administrative and
practical requirements for polling facilities (adequate parking and interior space, enough working
electrical outlets, etc.) result in few options for many precincts — and this is before any
consideration of property owners' willingness to host a voting precinct. In short, the legal and
administrative requirements for polling facilities result in very few options.

Engagement/Communication Tools
This change will be communicated to affected voters (approximately 37,000) in several ways.
First, information in the Qualified Voter File will be updated. This information will be available
to City staff, including 311 agents, and to voters directly through the Michigan Voter
Information Center. Second, the Election Division will send each affected voter a new Voter
Identification Card that highlights the polling location and/or precinct number change. Third, for
the elections in 2026 and 2027, signs will be posted at former polling facilities, directing voters
to their new polling locations. Fourth, the city’s website will be updated so that precinct maps,
driving directions, and street guides contain current information. Finally, the City Clerk's Office
will provide the updated precinct and polling location information to partners, like the County
Clerk's Office.

FISCAL IMPACT:

Fiscal Impacts Directly Related to this Action

Noticing Costs
Approximately 37,000 voters will be sent new Voter ID Cards at a cost of approximately
$13,000. The funds for this expense are included in the Election Division’s FY2026 Budget.

Polling Facility Rental Costs
In 2026 the City will pay $3,400 per election to rent polling facilities. This amount represents a
$200 decrease (per election) compared to prior years, as Precincts 15 and 23 will be moving to
facilities the City currently uses. The cost for the Precinct 10 polling facility will be available
once a location is identified. The property rental fees are included in the annual operating budget
for the Elections Division.

Fiscal Impacts Resulting from Precinct Combinations (Not Polling Facilities)

Operational Costs
Having seven fewer precincts will result in lower operational costs. The reductions will come in
the form of fewer staff hours needed to test equipment and pack supplies and lower poll worker
costs. It is difficult to anticipate the cost savings from reduced staff time for equipment and
supply preparation. It is estimated the City will realize a savings of approximately $14,000 in
poll worker costs for a November, even-year election and $10,000 for an August, even-year
election.

Future Capital Costs
The City will need to replace its voting equipment in 2027. The Michigan Bureau of Elections
has released a Request for Proposal (RFP) for precinct tabulators, accessible voting devices, and
high-speed ballot tabulators. The Bureau has advised local clerks to plan on spending $15,000
per precinct for this equipment and to not count on any state funding. Based on this cost
estimate, the City will save $105,000 by reducing the number of precincts from 27 to 20.
