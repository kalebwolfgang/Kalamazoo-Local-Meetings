City Commission Retreat – Strategic Planning Next Steps
The annual City Commission Planning Retreat, held Saturday at the KRESA Career Connect Campus, provided
dedicated time for Commissioners to discuss good governance, priorities, strategic goals and major initiatives.
An important goal was to seek alignment between the Commission, the community’s long-term vision.
Appreciation to the team of staff who worked diligently ahead of and during the meeting to make the process
work.

Following Saturday’s retreat, the next step in the process is to gather individual commissioner feedback on the
draft Vision Statement and the community-driven strategic concepts discussed. To support this, I will distribute
the Strategic Goal Alignment Survey to commissioners this week, with a request that responses be submitted by
Friday, March 20, 2026.

The survey is intended to capture individual input on areas of alignment with the draft Vision Statement and
whether the strategic concepts within each goal area should be adopted as written, refined, or expanded.
Responses are for individual input only, and no collective Commission discussion or decision will occur based
on the survey feedback until the framework is returned for consideration at a future public meeting for
discussion and potential adoption at that time. Staff will compile and summarize the responses for Commission
consideration.

Summer Festivals at Arcadia Creek Festival Place
Two weeks ago, I provided an update that Arcadia Creek Festival Place was the desired major event location
and that event planners and the DDA/DEGA committee were meeting to discuss opening the ACFP for summer
events. After evaluating several options, the Arcadia Creek Festival Place was deemed the most viable and
desirable location. As a result, this summer, downtown festivals are scheduled to return to Arcadia Creek
Festival Place even as the City prepares for the site’s planned renovation later this year:

• Kalamazoo Pride: June 5–6
• Caribbean Festival: June 26–27
• Kalamazoo Valley Blues Festival: July 10–11 (location subject to organizer confirmation)
• Canadiana Fest: September 19

The planned use of the ACFP was announced on the City’s website last week.

Background: Two years ago, after considering several site possibilities with event organizers, staff explored
relocating events to Mayor’s Riverfront Park, but organizers strongly preferred to remain downtown. Several

City of Kalamazoo Seeks Small Business Applicants for MEDC Match on Main Grant
If you’re a Downtown small business owner, you may be eligible to apply for a chance to receive up to $25,000
in grant funding through the Michigan Economic Development Corporation (MEDC) Match on Main program.
Match on Main is a competitive reimbursement grant program that supports new or expanding small businesses
located in vibrant downtown districts across Michigan. The City of Kalamazoo may apply to the MEDC on
behalf of up to two eligible businesses, each of which may receive up to $25,000 if the application is successful.

Businesses interested in being considered must meet the following criteria:

• They are located within the City of Kalamazoo.
• Operate within a traditional downtown, historic neighborhood commercial corridor, or an area zoned for
  concentrated commercial development.
• Operate from a physical storefront and sell goods or services directly to customers.
• Have, or obtain, site control prior to application submission.
• Be a for-profit or nonprofit organization headquartered in Michigan.
• Provide the required 10% cash match.

Interested businesses must complete a pre-application form and download the MEDC Local Business
Worksheet, both available on the City’s website at www.kalamazoocity.org/MatchOnMain. Completed
worksheets must be submitted to downtown@kalamazoocity.org by Friday, March 27, 2026, at 5:00 p.m. Late
submissions will not be considered. For more information about the Match on Main program and eligibility
requirements, visit: www.miplace.org/small-business/match-on-main.

Awards of Merit to Recognize Excellence in Historic Preservation Across Kalamazoo
The Kalamazoo Historic Preservation Commission (HPC) is now accepting nominations for the 2026
Historic Preservation Awards of Merit, recognizing individuals, organizations, and projects that make
meaningful contributions to preserving Kalamazoo’s historic places. Historic preservation is about more
than buildings; it’s about the people who care for them. These awards highlight the commitment, creativity,
and ongoing effort required to rehabilitate historic structures, maintain them over time, and ensure they
continue to serve the community.

Community members are invited to submit nominations in the following categories:

• Individual or Institutional Stewardship Award: Recognizes individuals or organizations whose ongoing
  preservation activities merit special recognition, or who have demonstrated excellence in the long-term care
  of a historic property.
• Place Award: Restoration, rehabilitation, or sympathetic infill construction demonstrating excellence in
  preservation. This can include creative adaptive reuse projects that incorporate sustainability, accessibility
  improvements, and thoughtful design solutions.

Nominations do not need to be located in historic districts. The HPC especially encourages nominations for
projects located outside of currently designated historic districts. Award recipients will be announced, and
awards presented during National Preservation Month in May. Nominations must be submitted online at
www.kalamazoocity.org/hpcawards through Tuesday, March 31.

Equitable Child Care Entrepreneurial Pillot Project Update
The Equitable Child Care Entrepreneurial Pilot Project continues to move forward as part of efforts to strengthen
access to quality childcare in the City of Kalamazoo while supporting family childcare providers as small
business owners. Building on the success of the previous Family Child Care cohort, the pilot continues to
provide business training, coaching, and technical assistance that help home-based providers strengthen and
expand their childcare programs while increasing access to care for Kalamazoo families.

Southwest Child Care Resources & Referral is partnering with All Our Kin, a nationally recognized leader in family
childcare business development, to bring proven training models and strategies to local providers. The project
also includes provider recruitment, program expansion support, and professional development opportunities
that connect Kalamazoo providers with broader learning networks. This investment helps to strengthen the
local childcare ecosystem while creating economic opportunities for providers who play a critical role in
supporting working families and the regional workforce.

Fire at Kalamazoo Metal Recycling
On the evening of Monday, March 9, 2026, our Kalamazoo Department of Public Safety (KDPS) responded
to a large fire at a metal recycling facility located in the 1500 block of Kings Highway. Crews arrived at
approximately 7:37 p.m. and encountered heavy flames and smoke coming from a large metal pile.
Firefighters worked throughout the night to contain the blaze, ultimately using more than one million gallons of
water before bringing the fire under control at approximately 4:00 a.m. on Tuesday, March 10.

I am pleased to report that the fire was contained to the facility and no injuries were reported. During the
incident, air monitoring identified temporarily elevated particulate levels overnight; however, those readings
returned to normal by 9:00 a.m. Tuesday morning. The Michigan Department of Environment, Great Lakes, and
Energy has been notified due to the potential for runoff into the Kalamazoo River. The cause of the fire remains
under investigation.

FIRE Funding Request to Support KDPS Alerting System Modernization
On Wednesday, March 11, 2026, I joined KDPS Assistant Chief Tibbets in Lansing to testify before the Michigan
House Labor and Economic Development Subcommittee in support of a legislatively directed spending request
of $400,000 to modernize the station alerting systems used by the Kalamazoo Department of Public Safety.

KDPS responds to approximately 14,000 fire, EMS, and rescue calls each year. However, four of our five stations
currently rely on outdated alerting systems that cannot target notifications to specific responders. As a result,
personnel are often unnecessarily awakened during 24-hour shifts when calls do not involve their apparatus or
assignment.

Research shows that chronic sleep disruption among firefighters is associated with increased risks of
cardiovascular disease, depression, fatigue-related injuries, and other long-term health concerns. The
requested funding would allow KDPS to install modern alerting systems in four stations and enable zone
notifications that alert only the personnel needed for a response. This upgrade would improve response
efficiency while also supporting the health, safety, and readiness of our first responders.

Southwest Michigan Tornado Response – KDPS Regional Support
Following the tornado that caused significant damage near Union City in Southwest Michigan on Friday, March
7, 2026, the 5th District Technical Rescue Team (TRT) was activated, and KDPS personnel were specifically
requested to assist with search operations.

Our team conducted systematic grid searches of heavily damaged structures under challenging conditions.
I am proud to report that KDPS personnel completed their assignment safely, and no additional victims were
located during the search operations.

In addition to this work, KDPS supported regional partners by deploying drone pilots to assist operations in Three
Rivers and by providing SWAT standby coverage for St. Joseph County during the incident.

These requests for assistance reflect the strong regional reputation and specialized capabilities of KDPS
personnel, as well as the department’s continued commitment to supporting neighboring communities during
major emergencies.
