RECOMMENDATION:

It is recommended that the City Commission approve an MDOT contract for transportation
enhancement activities under the Transportation Alternatives Program, specifically the Safe
Routes to Schools program. The project involves infrastructure improvements on Winchell
Avenue and Howard Street and serves as the City's match to leverage federal funds to cover the
majority of construction costs in the amount of $47,367.00.

BACKGROUND:

SRTS: Winchell Elementary and Maple Magnet Middle Schools Background.
In 2019 and 2020, the City was selected by the state to have Safe Routes to School Plans (SRTS) created for
Winchell Elementary School and Maple Magnet Middle School. Engagement brought together school
principals and staff, students, parents, and community members to discuss improvements that would make it
safer for students, particularly those who walk or bike to school. Engagement activities included walking
audits, meetings, and classroom activities. The SRTS report yielded many projects, large and small. The City
was able to incorporate many items from the report into already planned projects, including the Oakland Drive
construction project (improved pedestrian crossing at Maple Street and Oakland Drive) and Safe Street for All
traffic-calming projects on Maple and Stearns Streets by Maple Magnet Middle School. In 2021, the City
applied for SRTS funding for two projects: the addition of sidewalk and crosswalks by Winchell Elementary
School and an improved pedestrian crossing of Howard Street to better connect Vine Neighborhood students to
the middle school. The application was successful; the engineering design work was done in 2022 and 2023,
with the plan for construction to occur in 2024. Permitting for the projects was delayed at the state level for
additional review work by the State Historic Preservation Office (SHPO) and the National Environmental
Protection Agency (NEPA). The state agencies completed their review in 2025, which allowed the Michigan
Department of Transportation (MDOT) to proceed with finalizing the plans and soliciting a contractor to do
the work. MDOT is reviewing the contractor bids now and is requesting that the City approve the contract
before the Commission this evening, which accounts for the City's match ($ 47,367) to the SRTS award
funding of $387,825.

SRTS: Winchell Elementary and Maple Magnet Middle Schools Today.
Regular conversations with the stakeholder groups occurred during the initial SRTS engagement, plan creation,
and before the project was delayed. Staff did not conduct regular conversations and check-ins during the delay
years. This lack of communication created confusion and led to requests for additional information and
questions on the designs for the Howard Street pedestrian crossing by stakeholders. At the last City
Commission meeting, the approval of this contract was delayed until March 16th for staff to provide
information and answer questions of concerned residents, talk to MDOT to better understand timing
constraints for this contract and the SRTS work, and consider if the 4-to-3 lane conversion project for Howard
Street planned for 2028 could be moved forward to 2027 and combined with the SRTS pedestrian crossing.

Since the last Commission meeting, City staff have reached out and talked with several residents in the two
school communities, the adjacent neighborhood associations of Oakland Drive, Winchell Avenue, and Vine,
and Kalamazoo Public Schools (KPS), including transportation staff. Two coffee and crosswalk open houses
were held at the Vine Neighborhood Association. At these meetings, attended by Vine youth and parents,
pedestrian safety supporters, and KPS, participants were able to speak directly with traffic engineers and
planning staff to dig into questions and concerns. The handout from the meeting is attached to this agenda item
and includes the timeline discussed for moving SRTS forward for the two KPS schools. In these conversations,
staff heard residents' concerns with the placement of the crossing and the traffic control device planned, among
other topics such as traffic speed on Howard, lighting, and signage. Staff committed to reviewing the crossing
plans this spring and summer, working with the community to make design updates.

Specifically, staff are committed to the following this spring and summer.

      • City will work to review the plans with KPS (both busing and Maple Middle School) and the
        neighborhood, considering such elements as pedestrian island design, crossing location, signage, and
        lighting.
      • Any design changes that result from this work will be handled as a change order with the Michigan
        Department of Transportation (MDOT)’s selected contractor.
      • Howard Street 4-to-3 lane conversion will be shifted from 2028 to 2027 to align the safe routes to
        school (SRTS) work to occur with the Howard Street redesign project, which allows work to reduce
        driver speeds and crashes on Howard Street while also improving pedestrian crossings.
      • City staff commits to continuous conversation with Vine residents and KPS between now and project
        implementation.

SRTS: Winchell Elementary and Maple Magnet Middle Schools Contract Completion and Next Steps.
We are all committed to a safe route to school crossing on Howard Street, connecting students from the Vine
Neighborhood to Maple Magnet Middle School. To achieve this goal, the first step is to complete the
contracting process with MDOT, which is the contract (detailed below), before the Commission for review and
approval. The subsequent steps for staff are to set a timeline for the design review and conversations with
Maple Magnet Middle School students and residents of Vine — outreach to the school to set up a conversation
with students has already occurred and a survey of Vine residents by a Vine resident was conducted and results
supplied to City staff — and continue to the design and budgeting work to shift the Howard Street project from
2028 to 2027. Changes to the planned Howard Street pedestrian crossing will be done through a change order
directly with the MDOT contractor team and with the Howard Street 4-to-3 lane conversion projects. Work on
these actions will begin immediately after tonight's action.

The SRTS project encompasses the following improvements along Winchell Avenue (Rambling Road to
Oakland Drive) and Howard Street at Merrill Street:

•   Installation of concrete sidewalks, curb and gutter, and curb ramps.
•   Tree removals, earthwork, and plantings.

Per the agreement, the City must provide the following at no cost to the project (excluded from the federal

The following provisions require careful attention; however, they are standard items included in all of our
MDOT funding agreements:

• Hazardous Materials: If unknown hazardous substances are discovered, the City is responsible for all
remediation and contractor delay costs if they are not deemed eligible by the FHWA. Refusal to pay can lead
to project termination.
• Lead Service Lines: The City is solely responsible for all costs related to replacing lead or galvanized
service lines encountered during the project.
• Maintenance Compliance: Failure to maintain the improvements (e.g., sidewalks, plantings) can disqualify
the City from future federal aid.
• Jurisdiction: The City retains exclusive jurisdiction and liability for its highways; MDOT assumes no
jurisdiction or tort liability by entering this contract.
