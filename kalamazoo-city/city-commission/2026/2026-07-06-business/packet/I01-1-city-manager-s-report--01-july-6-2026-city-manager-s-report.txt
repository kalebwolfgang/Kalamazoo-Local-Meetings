Data Centers
The City of Kalamazoo continues to monitor the issue of data centers, including those impacting communities
here in southwest Michigan. There are many unanswered questions regarding the pros and cons of data
centers, and municipalities around the country are seeking to understand more about them and weighing
whether and how to regulate the projects being built. The City’s Information Technology team has put
together some facts and frequently asked questions regarding data centers, including the following:

What is a data center?
    • A data center is a building that houses computer servers, storage systems, and networking equipment.
    • Data centers support a wide range of everyday uses – cloud storage for photos and documents, video
         streaming, video games, business applications, financial transactions, and more.
    • Data centers vary enormously in size – from small commercial facilities of a few thousand square feet to
         hyperscale campuses covering hundreds of acres.
    • Artificial intelligence is one use of data centers, but it is not the only one. Most data centers serve many
         purposes simultaneously.
    • Data centers operate 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, and require continuous power and cooling to
         function.
Frequently Asked Questions
Q: How much electricity would a data center use?
A: Electricity use depends heavily on the size and design of the facility. The State of Michigan requires energy
producers to meet 50% clean energy by 2030, 80% by 2035, and 100% by 2040, which would affect the long-
term greenhouse gas implications of any facility’s energy use.
Q: How much water would a data center use?
A: Water use varies significantly depending on the cooling technology. Air-cooled or “dry” systems use
relatively little water. Evaporative or cooling-based systems can use substantially more – potentially millions of
gallons annually in large facilities.
Q: How noisy would a data center be?
A: The primary sources of noise from a data center are HVAC and cooling systems. Design choices, building
insulation, and site screening can all affect noise levels for nearby properties.
Q: What are the economic considerations:
A: The most direct long-term economic benefit would be increased property tax revenue. Data centers also
have substantial utility demands that may require infrastructure investment; depending on how those are
funded, they could create additional capacity that benefits other users. Under Michigan law, the cost of data
center electrical infrastructure would not be passed to other utility customers – data centers must pay 100% of
the energy they use, plus an exit fee if they leave.

Medicaid Eligibility Changes
In July 2025, Congress passed H.R. 1, a federal budget reconciliation bill that includes new work requirements
for certain Medicaid recipients enrolled through the Affordable Care Act’s Medicaid expansion. The State of
Michigan is expected to implement these requirements later this year for applicable populations.

Under the anticipated changes, affected individuals will be required to document approximately 80 hours per
month of qualifying activities—such as employment, education or job training, volunteer service, or other
activities defined by the Michigan Department of Health and Human Services (MDHHS), to maintain eligibility.
MDHHS will be responsible for administering the program and communicating directly with beneficiaries
regarding eligibility, reporting requirements, exemptions, and implementation timelines.

While program administration rests with the State, the City has a supporting role in helping ensure residents are
aware of significant changes and connected to accurate information and resources. City communications will
be aligned with MDHHS guidance to avoid confusion and ensure consistency with official State messaging.

City staff are reviewing MDHHS materials, coordinating with State partners as additional guidance becomes
available, and assessing appropriate ways to support public awareness. MDHHS has made the following
resources available:

   •   MDHHS work requirements webpage

   •   H.R. 1 Medicaid Implementation webinar recording

   •   Community Partner Communications Toolkit: community partner toolkit

SBA/FEMA Financial Assistance for Kalamazoo Flooding
The State of Michigan has informed the City of Kalamazoo that the City will receive disaster assistance through
the U.S. Small Business Administration (SBA) for residents, businesses, and nonprofit organizations impacted by
the April flooding. President Trump has approved the Major Disaster Declaration for certain areas of Michigan
impacted by the severe weather in April, and the City of Kalamazoo was included in the approved declaration
for Individual Assistance related to the flooding. This declaration makes FEMA Individual Assistance available to
eligible Kalamazoo residents who were impacted by the flooding. In addition, the SBA will also offer disaster
assistance to eligible homeowners, renters, businesses, and private nonprofit organizations.

At this time, an opening date for a tentative SBA Disaster Outreach Center serving the City of Kalamazoo,
Allegan County, and Barry County that would be located at the Gun Plain Fire Department in Plainwell, has
been postponed. We are currently coordinating with the State of Michigan, FEMA, SBA, and neighboring
emergency managers to determine the updated date and operation schedule. As soon as those details are
finalized, we will provide the information so it can be shared with the public.

Downtown Kalamazoo Business Receives MEDC Grant
The State of Michigan has awarded The Grazing Table, a restaurant located on the downtown Kalamazoo
Mall, with a $17,233 grant through the Michigan Economic Development Corporation (MEDC) and the MEDC’s
Match on Main program. The State of Michigan this year awarded over $1.5 million in grants to small local
business through the Match on Main program. The Match on Main program serves as a tool to support new or
expanding place-based businesses as they launch and grow in core downtown areas and commercial districts.
The program provides up to $25,000 in funding to support an eligible small business through a competitive
application process. This year’s application window saw a total of 144 applicants from 83 Michigan
communities – the highest participation rate in the program’s history.

The Grazing Table is located at 123 E. South Street on the downtown mall. The business sought Match on Main
funding to support improvements, such as purchasing additional refrigeration units to support food preparation
to enhance its operation capacity, as well as upgrades to its exterior façade to create a more welcoming
storefront, to enhance their customer experience. Information on the MEDC and Match on Main program can
be found here.

Kalamazoo Avenue Start of Construction
The long-awaited reconstruction and two-way conversion of Kalamazoo Avenue starts this month. The City of
Kalamazoo is planning a groundbreaking this month along the northwest corner of Park Street and Kalamazoo
Avenue to mark the start of the project. More details on the groundbreaking will be announced soon. The
community is invited to attend the event.

The Kalamazoo Avenue reconstruction and two-way conversion, along with the eventual conversion of
Michigan Avenue, West Main Street, Douglas Avenue, Lovell Street, and South Street in downtown Kalamazoo,
are part of the City’s broader Streets for All initiative to modernize our downtown infrastructure. The Kalamazoo
Avenue project represents a long-term investment in Kalamazoo’s future, replacing aging underground
infrastructure while redesigning the street to better serve residents, businesses, and visitors for generations to
come. The City of Kalamazoo has also posted a video on Streets for All to learn about the vision behind the
project, the history that led Kalamazoo to this point, and how these investments will help shape the next
chapter of Kalamazoo’s history. Watch the short video here.

Kzoo Park Rules
Kalamazoo’s city parks are the perfect setting for events as well as simply having fun. The City of Kalamazoo
encourages the peaceful and festive gathering of residents and non-residents, including visitors to our great
community, to the City’s many parks and trails. Utilization of the parks by organizations as well as large
gatherings are handled through the City’s special event permitting process. That process helps coordinate City
services, ensures organizers are aware of applicable park rules and operational requirements, and facilitates
communication among City departments in advance of larger public gatherings. The City lists the following
rules to make sure everyone can enjoy the parks.

   •   Parks are open daily from 8 a.m. to sunset.
   •   Parks remain open year round, however vehicle gates at some parks close on October 1 until May.
   •   Restrooms and water fountains are open Memorial Day through Labor Day (they may be available by
       request outside of this window for approved events, if possible).
   •   Always be respectful to other park visitors, park property, and facilities.
   •   Help keep our parks beautiful by disposing of trash properly in trash bins. If you see litter, lend a hand
       and toss it in a bin.
   •   Fires are only permitted for grilling food and only in provided picnic grills.
   •   Vehicles are only permitted in designated parking areas or roadways.
   •   Trees, wood, or plants cannot be removed.
   •   Pets must be kept on a six-foot leash.
   •   Alcohol is not permitted.
   •   Loud music is prohibited.
   •   Public Safety enforces all City Code of Ordinances.

Boil Water Advisories
Boil Water Advisories are precautionary. They are issued while we test to confirm the water has not been
affected. Our team completes two separate rounds of testing before advisories are lifted. While under a Boil
Water Advisory, affected customers are advised to boil their tap water for two minutes before consuming it, or
use bottled water until the advisory is lifted. No special actions are needed for water used for hygiene. The
vast majority of Boil Water Advisories are issued out of an abundance of caution and lifted within three days.

The Kalamazoo Department of Public Services hand delivers water advisory notices to affected homes and
businesses whenever possible. If the affected area is large and includes a lot of properties, advisories may be
issued by media release. Here is a list of watermain breaks and boil water advisories for the weeks of June 15-
